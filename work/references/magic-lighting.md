# Renderer magic pass: lighting references

Chatdom target: a handmade toy kingdom at dusk, with cool blue-violet surroundings and warm windows as people; retain readable voxel forms, controlled source-sized bloom, horizon fog, and reserve shafts for golden hour/beacon. See [Artstyle-Soul-Feel.md](../../chatdom/Artstyle-Soul-Feel.md) §§1–3, 6, 16.2.

## Transferable techniques

- **HDR and bloom:** keep scene lighting in a linear HDR buffer; extract/downsample bright light, blur it and add it back to the HDR scene before the final tone-map/output transform. The official Three.js post-processing guide documents scene → bloom → `OutputPass`, with output tone mapping last ([guide](https://threejs.org/manual/pages/post-processing.html)); its renderer supports half-float output for HDR/post-processing ([docs](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/WebGLRenderer)). Chatdom already specifies HDR/Karis bloom; keep the glow restrained so windows stay points and the beacon stays a beam, not a white veil. Complementary exposes bloom separately and modulates bloom fog by view distance and conditions ([source](https://github.com/ComplementaryDevelopment/ComplementaryReimagined/blob/main/shaders/lib/atmospherics/fog/bloomFog.glsl)).
- **Atmosphere and grade:** use height-dependent fog mixed toward the dusk sky colour, increasing with distance; keep nearby contrast and material hue. Three.js documents exponential fog and a height-fog factor in TSL ([FogExp2](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/FogExp2.html), [height fog](https://threejs.org/docs/pages/TSL.html)). Complementary's public shader config separates fog distance/altitude and sunset shaft saturation ([source](https://github.com/ComplementaryDevelopment/ComplementaryReimagined/blob/main/shaders/lib/common.glsl)). **Inference:** apply any restrained time/biome grade after scene effects but before output encoding; separable controls ease golden-street tuning.
- **Shafts:** use sparingly in authored views (beacon, low sun through gaps). Prefer low-resolution radial/screen-space shafts with depth/occlusion rejection, temporally stable filtering and tight intensity limits; reserve volumetric raymarching for high tier. Do not put shafts on every emissive window. Complementary exposes distinct day/night/rain shaft controls in its config ([source](https://github.com/ComplementaryDevelopment/ComplementaryReimagined/blob/main/shaders/lib/common.glsl)).
- **AO and voxel contact:** retain the existing mesh AO/contact term; add large-scale sky/voxel occlusion for eaves and alleys instead of making screen-space AO carry all depth. Teardown creator Dennis Gustafsson describes its ray-traced AO, soft shadows and specular occlusion, and explicitly says it does not implement GI ([interview](https://80.lv/articles/teardown-developer-breaks-down-multiplayer-and-voxel-destruction-tech)). **Inference:** Teardown's convincing voxel lighting need not imply costly full GI; Chatdom's authored/baked sky occlusion plus selective local lights fits its device tiers.
- **Particles:** use few, authored, slow particles (smoke, fireflies, celebratory sparks), emissive only where they signify a source; keep count, screen coverage, lifetime and overdraw bounded. **Inference from Chatdom's “calm by default, spectacular on cue” art decision:** effects density should follow moment intensity, not run continuously.
- **Performance/fallbacks:** budget effects independently by tier; lower bloom resolution/sample count, shaft quality, shadow distance and far detail before removing warm windows, horizon fog, or material readability. In his own renderer experiments, Teardown creator Dennis Gustafsson reports half-resolution diffuse lighting with edge-aware upscaling ([post](https://blog.voxagon.se/2017/11/09/upscaling-half-resolution-screen-space.html)); he notes artifacts and the need to protect edges, so this is a candidate for blurry lighting/AO, not a blanket resolution drop. His voxel-space post says coarse traversal is suitable for AO/volumetric fog, while exact traversal is reserved where needed, with mip levels used to accelerate rays ([post](https://blog.voxagon.se/2018/10/17/from-screen-space-to-voxel-space.html)). These are his engine experiments, not a guarantee for Chatdom's WebGL renderer. Chatdom's device-specific keep/drop rules are in [Quality.md](../../chatdom/Quality.md) §4.
