# Reference: Ultra Water

[Humpalumps/Ultra-Water](https://github.com/Humpalumps/Ultra-Water) · MIT · studied at `ce9248fa20afbdf63fd4c11709f68d7368aca0a0`

For: Chatdom's water rendering and shoreline. Source paths and line numbers below refer to the `ultra-water` reference checkout at this revision.

## What it is

A three.js WebGL2 ocean and island demo: FFT waves, refractive shallows, foam, shoreline breakers, planar reflections, caustics and underwater rendering. Its README describes seven camera shots and measured hardware results (`README.md:10-50`); the checkout contains no demo screenshots, and we did not execute its code. Its numbers are the author's measurements, not Chatdom benchmarks.

## What we take

- **Stable waves and roughness:** four 256² Tessendorf cascades at 600, 85, 12 and 2.1 m packed into one atlas; 16 Stockham IFFT stages with two half-float MRT outputs; time and frequencies loop at 300 s to avoid floating-point phase drift (`src/water/OceanSim.js:4-19`, `:236-253`, `:304-347`). JONSWAP wind and swell, directional spreading and gravity-capillary dispersion are in `:58-100`; defaults include wind 9 m/s, fetch 120 km, choppiness 0.85 and capillary fade 0.08 (`:205-225`). Those are an ocean calibration, not lake defaults. The finest band contributes slopes only. Store slope and squared slope so mip-filtered variance can become roughness; add geometric normal variance to suppress specular shimmer (`:180-194`, `src/water/OceanSurface.js:337-362`).
- **Continuous surface:** camera-centred expanding polar rings, 384 angular segments, outer radius 3 km and 60 km skirt; displacement selects mip by vertex spacing to avoid swimming (`src/water/OceanSurface.js:5-21`, `:242-258`). The high-camera half-density grid is a discrete change (`:8-9`, `:623-624`): Chatdom must hide or fade any transition. This geometry is a candidate for an open sea, not a required solution for bounded lakes.
- **Grounded shore:** a precomputed depth/distance/travel-time field uses fast-sweeping eikonal fronts at `sqrt(g * depth)`; it bends and slows breakers with bathymetry (`src/water/shoreMap.js:75-89`, `:112-165`). Crossfade ocean swell out in shallow water, then limit shore-wave height and horizontal compression; foam follows the breaking phase (`src/water/OceanSurface.js:143-213`). Shoreline contact foam uses thickness but is restricted to shallows to avoid white frills around floating props (`:413-430`). For small, placid lakes start with restrained edge motion, not ocean breakers.
- **Reflection and clear shallows:** render selected scene layers from a mirrored, oblique-clipped camera into a transparent mipmapped target, then composite over the sky; use roughness to select its mip (`src/engine/PlanarReflection.js:3-25`, `:27-54`; `src/water/OceanSurface.js:447-461`). Refracted scene colour is depth-validated and eased back at foreground edges, with Beer–Lambert absorption for thickness (`:413-425`). These make a useful basis for the required warm, shimmering window pillars, but the reference has no dedicated many-window pillar solution: prototype that separately, including its fallback.
- **Foam and caustics when useful:** foam follows the displacement Jacobian with frame-time recovery (`src/water/OceanSim.js:180-194`); the shore's world-space flow phases crossfade and erode as they age (`src/water/OceanSurface.js:500-536`). Caustics splat refracted triangle area ratios, use cubic B-spline slopes and multisample narrow folds; near/deep targets are 512² and 256² (`src/water/Caustics.js:5-34`, `:112-132`). This is optional expense for a visible shallow bed, not a requirement on every lake.
- **Queries and ordering:** render wave heights into a small GPU target and read asynchronously with layout tags; results are 1–3 frames old and stale layouts must be rejected (`src/water/WaterQuery.js:5-8`, `:39-58`). Don't use delayed visual samples as authoritative gameplay collision. Simulation, caustics, query, reflection, opaque and water passes are ordered in `src/main.js:351-403`; reflection and underwater passes are conditional.

## What we do differently

Water serves a crafted clay diorama and the shared palette, not a photoreal tropical sea: clear shallow edges, deeper water, restrained foam, and **warm pillars for each lit window at night**, with lilac/gold dusk and cool night surrounding the life (`chatdom/Artstyle-Soul-Feel.md:171-195`). Keep the horizon, avoid blown-out highlights, shimmer, seams and detail pops (`chatdom/Quality.md:140-171`). Tune geometry, colour and movement in actual kingdom shots at noon, dusk and night; source values are starting evidence, not an art prescription.

The source's phone profile sets render scale 0.6 × DPR (DPR capped at 2), 2× MSAA, reflection scale 0.35, and disables 12-pass bloom (`src/main.js:35-41`, `:106-130`). Its adaptive controller steps by 0.05 to 0.5 when >4% of frames miss, recovers after clean windows, and caps at 30 at the floor if >25% miss (`:518-541`). Coarse pointer is only a heuristic, not a performance test. The author's OnePlus Nord 4 results are 60 fps on these shots, with substantial thermal/device caveats (`README.md:32-50`). Profile actual devices; keep window lights, the clay look and horizon, and target 30 fps minimum on phones, 60 on laptops and 1080p stream (`chatdom/Quality.md:319-325`). Prefer scaling costly passes and effects before losing the night reflections; do not transplant the demo's permanent 30 fps cap without recovery or its resolution floors without visual testing.

## Pitfalls and compatibility

- WebGL2 / three.js, not WebGL1: GLSL3, multiple render targets, half/float render targets, float depth, mipmapped float textures, MSAA and asynchronous readback are used (`src/water/OceanSurface.js:616-622`, `src/water/OceanSim.js:236-253`, `src/main.js:111-117`, `src/water/WaterQuery.js:21-50`). WebGL2 alone does not guarantee all required float rendering/filtering, blending or multisample combinations: probe extensions and framebuffer completeness on real target devices and provide a visually coherent reduced path. Timer queries are optional (`src/engine/gpuTimer.js:1-6`).
- The reference admits no wakes, no floater–floater collisions, no curling tubes and coarse near-lens waterline geometry (`README.md:143-148`). A first-order shore solver rounds fronts at about one texel and under-resolves the waterline (`src/water/shoreMap.js:75-83`); terrain edits would require updating shore data. Avoid copying its expensive open-ocean assumptions blindly into a small evolving voxel kingdom.
- The author reports 0.45 ms FFT and 1.66 ms water for one desktop breaker shot, plus opaque/post/caustics (`README.md:14-28`); passes and scene content differ from Chatdom, so budget and benchmark the whole scene, not the isolated headline fps. Floater queries swallow read errors (`WaterQuery.js:50-58`); our implementation should detect failures and recover.
