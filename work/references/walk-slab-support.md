# Reference: standing on a stepped slab

For: the walking collision controller and founded-world collision compiler. Studied from checked-out sources; no demo, installation or live reproduction was run. Voxelize is a physics comparison, not a Colyseus project.

## What the references do

- [Voxelize physics engine](https://github.com/voxelize/voxelize/tree/7ae1c202c4ed33e6f18ea2e947c5c6f9a373b605/packages/physics-engine) · MIT · `7ae1c202c4ed33e6f18ea2e947c5c6f9a373b605`: actual world-space voxel AABBs, including partial boxes. Its [collision and auto-step](https://github.com/voxelize/voxelize/blob/7ae1c202c4ed33e6f18ea2e947c5c6f9a373b605/packages/physics-engine/src/index.ts#L736-L880) reset contact axes, sweep, then try a scratch-box step only on ground and against a blocked horizontal axis. It finds the obstacle's actual top, sweeps up with headroom, replays horizontal motion, requires progress, and sweeps down. Height slop is 0.001 m and the default graze ratio 4 (~14°); its engine epsilon and slop are implementation choices, not a recommendation to enlarge Chatdom's tolerances blindly. [Top lookup](https://github.com/voxelize/voxelize/blob/7ae1c202c4ed33e6f18ea2e947c5c6f9a373b605/packages/physics-engine/src/index.ts#L675-L688) includes an optional taller barrier.
- [voxel-aabb-sweep](https://github.com/fenomas/voxel-aabb-sweep/tree/031a033d724cbf1a60b3ad9fb8ea37dcbedfa807) · MIT · `031a033d724cbf1a60b3ad9fb8ea37dcbedfa807`: [leading-corner traversal and leading-face collision](https://github.com/fenomas/voxel-aabb-sweep/blob/031a033d724cbf1a60b3ad9fb8ea37dcbedfa807/index.js#L21-L73) sweeps continuously rather than checking only the final point; its grid-solid API alone does not represent arbitrary half-height boxes. [voxel-physics-engine](https://github.com/fenomas/voxel-physics-engine/tree/53685b1219eba404fbf7ad35216f8c593ed0db41) · MIT · `53685b1219eba404fbf7ad35216f8c593ed0db41` [records collision axes and trials a rise](https://github.com/fenomas/voxel-physics-engine/blob/53685b1219eba404fbf7ad35216f8c593ed0db41/src/index.js#L320-L391), but rounds the rise to the next integer voxel, so do not copy that assumption for slabs.
- [Colyseus core and SDK](https://github.com/colyseus/colyseus/tree/724b5449b51dcc07b7835afd7b3e4898fda2c7f7) · MIT · `724b5449b51dcc07b7835afd7b3e4898fda2c7f7`: [fixed timestep](https://github.com/colyseus/colyseus/blob/724b5449b51dcc07b7835afd7b3e4898fda2c7f7/packages/core/src/Room.ts#L965-L1016) ties one input to one tick, shares `dt` and optional substeps with prediction, and warns against acknowledging inputs never simulated. [Reconciler](https://github.com/colyseus/colyseus/blob/724b5449b51dcc07b7835afd7b3e4898fda2c7f7/packages/sdk/src/predict/reconciler.ts#L30-L52) replays the same step and smooths visual error; [wire-precision comparison](https://github.com/colyseus/colyseus/blob/724b5449b51dcc07b7835afd7b3e4898fda2c7f7/packages/sdk/src/predict/reconciler.ts#L293-L319) avoids a correction when predicted and authoritative fields encode identically. Neither resolves bad ground geometry.
- [Colyseus prediction playground](https://github.com/colyseus/prediction-playground/tree/90d7dd247158d18a1d9b024ecc1087a7de5ddf2e) · MIT · `90d7dd247158d18a1d9b024ecc1087a7de5ddf2e`: [shared movement](https://github.com/colyseus/prediction-playground/blob/90d7dd247158d18a1d9b024ecc1087a7de5ddf2e/src/shared/movement.ts#L6-L48) is a clean server/client deterministic example, but is a 2D arena clamp, not a slab or tile solver. The supplied Colyseus monorepo's `PREDICTION.md` mentions a separate hand-rolled platformer, but its `test/prediction` source is absent in that checkout. The supplied Defold platformer uses a separate engine and is not evidence of a shared 3D slab solver. No applicable Colyseus tile auto-step example was found in these sources.

## Chatdom: observed facts and diagnostic implications

`packages/shared/src/realm/movement.ts:4-8,23-31` defines 30 Hz, a 0.28 m radius, 1.75 m height, 0.5 m step and an immutable world-space box query per revision. `packages/world/src/movement/step.ts:51-75` refuses mismatched revisions, recovers overlap, sweeps vertically first, then slides/steps, steps down, and recomputes grounding at the final position. `collide.ts:225-268` trials rise, horizontal travel, settle and support before commit; support requires horizontal footprint overlap and a solid top within 0.000001 m below feet. This is the right kind of final-box test, not merely an origin-point test. The landing test also asks for a tread 0.2 m beyond the face, while movement is permitted from any positive footprint overlap.

The broad-phase contract says boxes *intersecting* bounds, but does not explicitly define inclusive face contacts. The supported probe extends below the feet and therefore overlaps a correct slab top by 0.000001 m even with a strict intersection query. By contrast, an upward sweep and point-shaped `treadAt` query have zero-width boundaries, where strict intersection or grid-cell rounding can omit contacts. The test fixture (`packages/testing/fixtures/kingdom-walk.ts:13-21`) uses inclusive comparisons; it is explicitly simulated, not evidence of the founded collision compiler. No concrete founded-world collision compiler was located in this checkout; `apps/web/src/kingdom/README.md:8` assigns that boundary to the scene provider. Check its actual transformed boxes and broad-phase behavior on both authority and predictor, including bounds exactly on slab faces and cell boundaries.

**Inference, not a demonstrated root cause:** a visible ascent followed by a downward correction may come from the final collision box differing from the rendered slab (height, transform, or missing top), an invalid support/vertical broad-phase result, a revision or authoritative reconciliation mismatch, or visual step easing. The source alone does not establish which. A revision mismatch in `stepPlayer` holds position rather than intentionally pushing down; do not assume it is the cause. Log the same fixed-tick feet, velocity, grounded, actual candidate boxes and revision on both sides before and after the correction. Compare predicted state with the authoritative ack separately from rendered/eased feet.

## One focused scenario

Use real compiled geometry: a floor at y=0 and a broad slab whose final world-space solid is `[1,0,-1]`–`[2,0.5,1]`; start grounded at `(0.5,0,0)` walking +X, no jump. At 30 Hz, let the body cross the face, stop, and idle several ticks. Expect the final box never to penetrate the slab, feet to settle and remain at y=0.5 with `grounded=true`, and authority and replay to agree at matching input acknowledgements. Query the actual collision revision for the swept body, the 1 µm support probe, and the tread point; inspect the returned slab top rather than substituting the hand-built test fixture. Repeat from a sufficiently clear jump landing to compare the user's contrasting path. This tests ascent **and sustained support**, not just one frame of upward movement.

## What we take / leave behind

Keep one shared fixed-tick law, real partial-box tops, full-body sweeps, bounded up/forward/down trials, headroom, actual progress and final support. Resolve the compiler's face-inclusion and world-coordinate contract explicitly and keep it identical per immutable revision. Do not blindly import unit-grid step height, Voxelize's tolerances, or a 2D arena clamp.
