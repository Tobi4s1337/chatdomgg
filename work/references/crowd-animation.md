# Reference: crowdbake

https://github.com/natepac/crowdbake · license unclear / no license found · studied at `1f6c8b44f0e1d57360503a935e120065ef0bd51b`

For: character animation and crowd rendering (`chatdom/Tech.md` §8.3–8.4). Source line references below are to the `crowdbake` reference checkout at that revision.

## What it is

A three.js/WebGL2 crowd experiment with offline baked animation, instanced rendering, chunk culling, LODs, cross-fades, stride matching, animated shadows and bone-driven attachments. Its useful contribution is the whole pipeline and the candid account of its bottlenecks, not proof that its crowd counts will meet Chatdom's frame budgets. No demo image is present in this checkout, and the untrusted demo was not executed; its visual quality has not been judged.

## What we take

- **Bake and sample.** Sample clips offline (default 30 Hz) into frames. Vertex animation textures (VAT) store a position and normal for every vertex and frame; a linear index `frame * vertexCount + vertexId` maps into 2D. Nearest `texelFetch` reads two frames and interpolates explicitly; normalized positions in fp16 and octahedral normals reduce memory. The separate bone mode stores three RGBA32F rows per bone and frame. See `README.md:33–100`, `tools/bake.mjs:127–150,203–246`, `src/vat/vat-shader.js:53–144`. Check actual `MAX_TEXTURE_SIZE` and texture support on each device: the README's 16384 example is not a guaranteed phone capability.
- **A compact instance record and a common clock.** Its 16 floats contain world transform, two clip/offset/rate sets, fade start, scale and appearance. Shader phase is `fract(offset + time * rate)`, so an unchanged cell needs no animation upload. A rate change adjusts offset to preserve phase; clip transitions phase-align and use two sampled poses. Completed fades must promote B into A or cheaper distant shaders resurrect the old clip. See `README.md:47–65,493–504`, `src/vat/VATCrowd.js:423–515,528–541`. Note that restarting a fade promotes its target immediately, which can jump; do not assume this is a satisfactory interruption policy for hero actions.
- **Plant feet.** The bake estimates stride as median backwards velocity of planted feet × clip duration; playback rate is speed / stride. `tools/lib/stride.mjs:71–150`, `src/vat/VATAsset.js:53–62,190–198`. This reference is speed-driven; Chatdom's distance-driven phase must integrate actual travelled distance, including corrections and stops, and preserve phase through transitions. Validate planted feet on slopes, turns, speed changes and interrupted actions, not only its procedural analytic test.
- **Cull and measure the actual work.** Counting-sort instances into bounded spatial cells, one sphere/draw/LOD per cell, gather and upload only dirty cells, and apply the same pose in shadow materials. Smaller cells improve culling but increase draws; an unculled far pool is a hardware-dependent trade-off. Retain original vertex IDs in simplified LOD geometry if using VAT. See `README.md:102–115,142–157,482–509`, `src/vat/VATCrowd.js:574–758`, `src/vat/VATAttachment.js:156–185`. Count submitted and post-cull instances, triangles including shadows, uploads, draw calls, CPU and GPU times separately. Measure wall clock rather than clamped simulation delta (`README.md:454–460`).
- **Accessories share pose and spatial grouping.** The example reads bone rows for a socket at both frames, applies its offset and uses the same per-chunk instance data for body and attachment; it collapses non-wearers to degenerate geometry (`src/vat/VATAttachment.js:1–11,38–94,172–185`). This still incurs a draw per accessory style and vertex work for non-wearers; it is not free. Prefer compact per-style instance lists where sparse wardrobes make that cheaper, and verify on devices.

## What we do differently

- **Bone textures fit the product.** Chatdom's shared ~14-bone skeleton has rigid voxel parts bound to one bone, not a fixed skinned mesh. A pose texture can drive each body part and wearable with one bone influence rather than the example's four-influence skin path; reuse poses across shapes and sockets. Compare actual bandwidth, shader work and memory against VAT on real devices before choosing layout. Matrix row interpolation in the example can shear or shrink during rotation; evaluate rigid rotation interpolation and hierarchy, especially for large motions and transitions. Near subjects use the specified CPU layered evaluator; GPU crowd posing must support coherent handoff without a visible pose or phase discontinuity.
- **Actual character, not random mannequins.** Seed tempo, posture, three of sixteen idle habits, blink rhythm, cheer and reaction delay from citizen ID and kingdom clock; reactions ripple with 80–350 ms jitter. The example uses `Math.random()` for initial phase and clip-switch throttling (`VATCrowd.js:424–429`, `crowd-sim.js:423–432`), not cross-client deterministic identity. Layer stance, masked upper-body actions, face and procedural motion as required by Tech §8.3; whole-body A/B fades alone cannot deliver a walking hammer action. Event start ticks and distance phase must agree across clients.
- **Faces, capes and sockets are first-class.** Bind face plates to the head; drive eyes, blinks, mouths and expressions with deterministic shader parameters, and swap expressions during blinks. Use the shared socket pose for hands, hat and back; spring chains and capsule collision for near capes/hair, with a tested, continuous lower-cost representation at distance. Preserve tier-3 cape's palette-ramp colour shimmer without added glow. A baked rigid cap is not a spring cape; verify accessories and shadows during transitions and under camera movement.
- **Choose tiers by projected size and relevance**, not only chunk-centre distance: Tech §8.4 requires 64/24/12/16 full characters for PC/laptop/phone/stream, then near, mid, box and lights tiers, with hysteresis and ~0.4 s dithered transitions. Phone maxima are 12 full, 60 near, 250 mid, 800 box and then lights; use the existing tiers as the phone fallback, keep important nearby subjects animated, no phone shadow map. Cull by spatial cell without letting its centre degrade a long-lens subject. Quality changes happen at cuts, doors or loads. Preserve a real person's identity and count; lurkers are lights only, not simulated bodies.
- **Budgets are scene-wide, not a crowd entitlement.** Phone target ≤20 ms GPU and ≤0.8M triangles including shadows, ≤900 MB tab; everyday laptop ≤12 ms / 2M / 1.2 GB; OBS ≤4 ms / 2M / 900 MB (Tech §8.5). Crowd plus landscape, lights, UI and effects share these limits. The stream shot keeps its detail, lights and animation; allowed dynamic resolution is down to ~75%. Test a nine-person hamlet and capital, day/dusk/night, long lenses and camera moves on real phone, laptop and OBS GPU, including upload spikes and context loss.

## Measurements and limitations

The README reports a 2,367-vertex, 25-bone asset with 288 frames: 7.80 MB vertex textures versus 337.5 KB bone data; bone mode uses ~24 fetches per vertex for four influences versus four for VAT (`README.md:69–78,138–157`). Those numbers describe this model, not Chatdom's one-bone parts. On a Ryzen 7 7735HS, renderer-free CPU medians are 0.42 ms for 1k, 2.14 ms for 10k and 17.58 ms for 100k; separation is disabled above 40k (`README.md:159–171`). This is neither a complete frame nor a phone measurement. The 20k example submits 1.78M triangles before frustum culling in its optimized configuration, already over the *whole* phone scene allowance (`README.md:173–178`). Its 4k-agent 9.73 fps is SwiftShader, not real GPU evidence (`README.md:189–195`). Far-pool driver benefits are conjectured in the README and need A/B measurement. There is no phone fallback or tested mobile performance documented; WebGL2 alone does not establish one. No runtime IK, retargeting or look-at, and no impostor tier (`README.md:522–538`).

## Leave behind

The demo's generic procedural humanoid, random schedules, distance-only chunk LOD, abrupt clip/LOD fallback, its automatic disabling of separation at large counts, and any claim that 100k bodies are playable on our target devices. Do not ship its example assets or treat its procedural crowd as Chatdom citizens. Chatdom's crafted voxel palette, readable silhouettes, grounded movement and no-pop quality rules govern the result.
