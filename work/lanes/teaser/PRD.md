## The part
A proper YouTube teaser for Chatdom, 60 to 90 seconds, that looks like real Twitch footage and makes a first-time viewer feel the idea in one watch: a chat becomes a people, the people get a kingdom, and kingdoms meet on a map of Twitch.

It is staged footage, shot like a film. Every frame is rendered, never screen-grabbed in real time:
- **The Twitch page studio** (`apps/studio`): a pixel-faithful recreation of tobi4s_'s Twitch channel page, built around the real Chatdom stream view in the player and the real Chatdom panel under it. The top bar, side bar, player, channel header, about and panels area, and chat all match the live Twitch page at 1920×1080. Chat replays scripted lines with real-looking timing: badges, name colours, emotes, replies, sub and raid notices, a Hype Train bar, the follow button, a viewer count that moves. A shot script drives all of it from one clock, so any frame of any scene renders the same every time. It is shot as if screen-recorded: a real cursor moving like a hand, theatre mode, a scroll, a click on the panel.
- **Kingdom footage at film quality:** every kingdom shot rendered frame by frame on Tobias's RTX 5080 through pc-lease, with the clock stepped exactly 1/60 s per frame, the renderer at its best settings, no dropped or doubled frames, at 1080p or higher (target 2160p).
- **The voice:** one warm storyteller, chosen from Tobias's three ElevenLabs candidates, who is also the Voice of the Realm. Short lines, room to breathe, timed captions from the alignment data.
- **Music and sound:** a score that swells chapter by chapter, the kingdom's own soundscape, and a few hero sounds (the forge hammer, the gate, the raid horn, the crowd).
- **The cut:** assembled with ffmpeg from an edit decision list in the studio, cut on the music's beats, captions burned in for muted viewing. A rough cut tonight from today's build, a first full cut at film quality, and the final cut re-shot on the build merged together on Monday 2 Nov.

The bar: someone who has never heard of Chatdom watches it muted on a phone and can say what it is. Someone who streams can't tell the Twitch page from a real recording. The kingdom shots stand next to the best stills in `work/evidence/grand-kingdom/` and look better, because they move.

## Decisions
1. **Staged, never labelled, never public.** The teaser is staged footage and carries no SIMULATED marks (decision 25). The studio is a private tool: 127.0.0.1 only, no public route, not in `scripts/public`, never linked. Fixture triggers live only in the studio's shot scripts.
2. **The replica is ours, measured from Twitch.** The page is our own HTML and CSS, built to match a reference capture of the real twitch.tv/tobi4s_ page, logged out, read only. Twitch's own web fonts and icons may be loaded into the local media folder at capture time for local rendering only. They are never committed and never served. The recorder never types or clicks on twitch.tv, and nothing is ever posted to Twitch.
3. **Identities.** The broadcaster is tobi4s_. The other named channels are the consenting Genesis channels The_AGGman, PinkKittyRose and MashaD4 (raids, the side bar, the Atlas road). Chatters are invented names checked against Helix Get Users so that none belongs to a real account. Other side-bar entries are invented in the same way. No real streamer's name, face or emotes appear unless that streamer consented. On the Atlas, only consenting channels are legible (see SEAMS.md for the ask to atlas-map). Emotes are Twitch's global emotes and the consenting channels' own emotes.
4. **Honest scale on the Twitch page.** tobi4s_ shows a small streamer's numbers (tens of viewers), and the PinkKittyRose raid shows 48. That is the product's message: at five viewers, a raid of three is the night's biggest moment. The huge-raid column (S16) is a world-only shot with no name and no number. Tobias can overrule this (see Backlog).
5. **Time is a pure function.** Every studio frame is f(shot, t). Nothing animates on its own: CSS animations are driven or seeked from the shot clock. The recorder injects a virtual clock before page scripts run (`Page.addScriptToEvaluateOnNewDocument` over CDP, which reaches same-origin frames). It advances exactly 1/60 s per frame, waits for the renderer to be idle, and captures. It never records in real time. Mesh and land workers are allowed to finish before each frame, so pop-in can't depend on host speed.
6. **Two plates, one composite.** Kingdom plates are rendered on the GPU. Page plates (DOM only) are rendered in the studio with the player area keyed. The studio compositor (or ffmpeg) puts the plate into the player at its exact pixel rectangle per frame. The push-through (S03) and the push-ins are a virtual camera over a 2160p composite. For the rough cut only, the player may run the live stream view in place.
7. **Capture on the GPU.** First choice is canvas capture encoded in the page with WebCodecs on Tobias's GPU (explicit timestamps, high bitrate), with chunks pulled back over CDP and muxed here. The fallback is `Page.captureScreenshot` (PNG at 1440p, high-quality JPEG at 2160p). Benchmark both in the first lease and record frames per minute in TAKES.md. Capture must not stall the clock: if a frame isn't settled, wait, never skip.
8. **60 fps, 2160p master, 1080p floor.** The page renders at 1920×1080 CSS pixels at device scale 2. Full-frame kingdom plates render at 3840×2160 (2560×1440 if the leases can't carry 4K), in-player plates at the player's pixel size, and nothing below 1920×1080.
9. **No signs in the raids.** In the world there are no cards, panels, captions or tableau UI (unlike the raid-road stills). The only text is Twitch's own raid notice in chat and the narration. A viewer seeing it for the first time must get "they came to us" and "we rode out to them" from the picture.
10. **The product's own pixels.** Every kingdom pixel comes from Chatdom's renderer and assets. Where the product can't stage a moment yet (the Way Home, the huge column, the rise to the map), the studio builds a set piece from the product's own modules by read-only import. No generated imagery and no painted frames for kingdom footage. Title cards use the carved kit and fonts from `packages/ui`, IM Fell English and Alegreya.
11. **The Way Home is staged.** No lane builds B03 this round, so S05 to S07 are staged from the road, gate, street, window lighting, own door and walker. If a lane lands the real Way Home before the shoot freeze, the final takes use it.
12. **One voice.** The narrator is the Voice of the Realm (J01), chosen from `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_CANDIDATES`. Generation uses the REST text-to-speech endpoint with timestamps. Compare the model that takes audio tags (eleven_v3) against the steadier eleven_multilingual_v2 on one line first, then commit to one. Three takes per final line; the alignment data drives the captions. Every request is cached by voice, model, settings and text, so nothing is paid for twice.
13. **Sound.** Ambience comes from the kingdom's own soundscape, rendered offline (see `work/evidence/soul/audio/` and `packages/audio`). Hits come from the CC0 sounds already credited in `THIRD_PARTY.md`, plus a few ElevenLabs sound effects (forge hammer, gate, raid horn, crowd, the whoosh through the screen). Twitch's own notification sounds are not used. The music source is Tobias's decision (Backlog); until then the rough cut uses a temporary bed that never ships.
14. **The edit is data.** `apps/studio/edit/` holds the edit decision list (shots, in and out frames, transitions, the caption track, the audio stems). One script renders it with ffmpeg. The same list renders the rough cut, the first cut and the final cut. diffusion.studio remains an option for Tobias's own pass.
15. **The studio package stays out of the product build.** `apps/studio/package.json` is private, has no `build` script (so `pnpm -r build` and `pnpm verify` skip it) and uses only dependencies already in the lockfile. Adding the workspace package changes `pnpm-lock.yaml`. Do that in one early, separate step and note it in progress.

## How to work
- **Checkpoints.** Each goal takes many sessions. When a session ends before a goal is done, leave everything building and working, don't tick the box, and append `next: <id> <what is done / what remains>` to `progress.txt`. Tick a goal only when its Done when is truly met. TS10 must be checkpointed by Monday morning, because the rough cut is the safety net.
- **Never run git.** Don't run commit, merge, rebase, reset, push, stash or checkout. A reviewer commits your work after each session and merges main into this branch when main moves. That is how the merged build arrives here on Monday.
- **The GPU lease is shared with seven lanes.** Use `chatdom-pc-lease --wait 10 --max 10 -- <command>` and never hold it longer than 10 minutes. Render in chunks: one shot or part of one per lease. Release the lease between chunks, and do CPU work (encoding, edit, sound) while others hold it. Inside a lease, serve the studio on 127.0.0.1:5192 and this worktree's web build on 5191 (never 5190). Outside a lease, never occupy 5191 or 5192, because other lanes' leases fail if those ports are busy. Use a free port from `packages/testing/capture-ports.mjs`, checked with `ss` first. If `pc-lease` exits 3, the tunnel is down: carry on with page plates, voice, sound and edit, and use real stills for kingdom stand-ins in the rough cut. Keep Tobias's Chrome tidy: one tab, closed at the end, and don't change his settings. If the window is minimized (`pc vis`), stop and write `needs: tobias un-minimise Chrome`.
- **The host is shared.** Page plates (DOM only) may render headless here: under `nice -n 19`, one Chrome, in chunks of at most 5 minutes, holding the shared capture lock per chunk. WebGL footage never renders on the CPU; SwiftShader is for single stills only, under the capture lock, and its timings mean nothing. Run every ffmpeg encode under `nice -n 19` with `-threads 4` or fewer. Build the web app niced and only when its source changed. Typecheck and test only `apps/studio`.
- **The ElevenLabs key.** It is loaded only from `~/.config/chatdom/elevenlabs.env` (`ELEVENLABS_API_KEY`, `ELEVENLABS_VOICE_CANDIDATES`), for example with `node --env-file=$HOME/.config/chatdom/elevenlabs.env apps/studio/voice/<script>.mjs`. Never print, echo, log or write it anywhere: not in the repo, the media folder, progress, error messages or request dumps. Scripts never log request headers.
- **Keep the ElevenLabs spend modest.** Keep a ledger in `work/lanes/teaser/voice-ledger.md` with the date, purpose, voice, model and characters (or credits) per batch, and nothing secret. Read the account's usage from the subscription endpoint before and after each batch. Stay under 40,000 credits for the whole lane. Above that, stop and write `needs: tobias raise the ElevenLabs budget`. Render a line once and reuse it from the cache. Compare voices on short lines before rendering takes.
- **Big media stays out of git.** Everything heavy lives in `/srv/chatdom/media/teaser/`: `reference/` (Twitch reference captures and fonts), `plates/`, `takes/`, `audio/` (voice, music, effects, stems), `cuts/` (masters). Delete frame sequences once a take's encode is verified. Keep the folder under 40 GB (the disk has about 128 GB free and is shared). `work/evidence/teaser/MEDIA.md` lists what is there, with sizes and SHA-256 sums. In git: the rough and first cuts as review encodes under 10 MB, the final cut at 1080p (about 25 MB), stills and contact sheets under 2 MB each, and small text (shot list, edit list, take log, ledger).
- **The seams.** Edit only `apps/studio/**`, `work/evidence/teaser/**` and `work/lanes/teaser/**`. Read anything. When you need something from another lane, write `needs: <lane> <exact ask>` in progress and in `SEAMS.md`, and work around it meanwhile: CSS or a shim in the studio's own frames, a set piece, or a stand-in still. Never patch their files. `SEAMS.md` lists what you read, from whom and when. On Monday, run the hooks check (`apps/studio/rig/hooks-check.mjs`, written in TS10) on the merged build first, and fix the shot scripts to match what changed.
- **Twitch.** Never write to Twitch. Reading public data is fine: Helix Get Users for name checks, and the logged-out reference capture of twitch.tv/tobi4s_.
- **Look at everything.** Watch every take as a contact sheet and every cut end to end, with and without sound, and say in progress what you still don't like. Stills go in `work/evidence/teaser/`.
- **Repo text.** Plain English. No thread ids, and no names of AI tools, models or orchestration in files you write. The ElevenLabs name is fine, because it is the product's voice.

- **Realm migrations:** each lane has its own number. raid-show `0011`, panel-window `0012`, play `0013`, forge-wish `0014`, grand-world `0015`. A lane that needs a second one appends a letter, for example `0012b`. If the migration runner needs contiguous numbers, the numbers are renumbered when the lanes are merged; never renumber another lane's file.
- **Raid roads:** pull, not push. raid-show owns the durable, append-only feed of witnessed raids, `GET /realm/raid-roads?after=&limit=`, with items that follow `AtlasRaidSchema` (`packages/shared/src/atlas-raids.ts`). atlas-map pulls from that feed with a cursor into its own road ledger, so it can catch up after a restart. atlas-map's `POST /v1/roads/observe` is optional and raid-show does not depend on it.
- **Contracts:** adding to `packages/shared` is allowed for every lane: new files and new optional fields, but no changed or removed exports. The realm modules for your own feature are yours (Forge modules for forge-wish, raid-gate for raid-show, the panel ingress for panel-window, look storage for play). Hooks into the realm core (rooms, lifecycle, server.ts, main.ts) are small, additive and typed, written up in your SEAMS.md. "Widen the contract" is not something to wait on main for.
- **Shared allowlists and configs:** `apps/api/src/public/routes.ts` (the public gateway allowlist, owned by front-door), `apps/web/src/main.tsx`, `apps/web/vite.config.ts` and package.json scripts take one additive line per route or registration from any lane. No reformatting.
- **SIMULATED:** front-door stops the shared kit from rendering any SIMULATED chip but keeps every exported name. Each lane removes the chips and fixtures in its own scope and updates its own tests. Leftovers are fixed when the lanes are merged.
- **Real GPU:** `chatdom-pc-lease` (its header is the manual) and `chatdom-pc shot|fps` with full `http://localhost:5192/...` URLs. Each lease is at most 10 minutes. Render long sequences in chunks across several leases.
- **Engine:** a lane run by an engine that commits its own work commits each checkpoint on its lane branch as `.agents/skills/ralph-lane/SKILL.md` says. A lane whose worker is told "never run git" leaves the commits to its reviewer. Both are fine, and only the instruction you were given applies to you.
