## The part
The grand kingdom: every kingdom is a whole voxel landscape you would screenshot.
Open it and orbit, and you see a place on the scale of the Eldermere reference (`/tmp/chatdom-refs/grand/grand-ref-1..6.jpg`, one browser voxel scene: about 118 million voxels, 205 homes, 3,065 trees, 73-84 fps drawing 5-6M of 19M quads):
- the channel's land rising out of the sea in tens of millions of voxels: mountain ranges with rock strata and snow by slope, cliffs, a mere, rivers and waterfalls, beaches, sea stacks;
- forests of distinct species (autumn gold, pine, cherry, a fantasy teal), patchwork fields with windmills, meadows;
- the castle on its rock with its spires and banners; a walled town where every citizen's home stands on a real street; the harbour with its ships and lighthouse;
- voxel clouds drifting, golden-hour light with long soft shadows, aerial haze to the horizon, a sea that reflects, and as the day turns, the windows lighting at dusk.
It runs smoothly in the browser on a good GPU (60+ fps at 1080p) and degrades gracefully on the software renderer. Genesis seeds all of it from the channel: land from the broadcaster id, biome and landmarks from its categories, crest colours on banners and roofs, emotes in stone and glass, sized hamlet to capital, so two kingdoms never look alike. Everything that exists keeps working inside it: homes and growth, citizens and walking, the Forge square, the moments, the raid road arrivals.
The bar: a still of it stands next to the Eldermere reference without embarrassment, and it is ours (our palette, our crest, our homes), not a copy.
You own it end to end and make the design calls. Be ambitious and think it through: the scale comes from the generation and the renderer together (far LOD, chunk streaming, greedy meshing, instancing for trees and props, GPU culling), not from hand-placing.
First proof: tobi4s_'s kingdom and the three Genesis channels (The_AGGman, PinkKittyRose, MashaD4) re-founded at this scale, with an orbit view at `/-/grand` and before/after stills of the same kingdom (today's view vs grand).
