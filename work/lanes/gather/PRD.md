## The part

There is always something cozy to do together. Tobias put it plainly: "what about stuff like campfire, tavern, minigames people can play together? this is also crucial, a cozy campfire scene would be awesome and minigames in tavern also (… we can just provide a few basic fun ones, also stuff like PONG and fun games like that)".

**The tavern door is a portal.** On the square stands the existing tavern, warm windows and smoke from its chimney. Crossing its door leaves the kingdom room and joins that kingdom's separate `tavern` room, covered by a short, beautiful transition. The session, look and identity carry across. Inside is its own small world, with its own scene, lighting and walkers, unconstrained by the exterior footprint. The light turns to paint: fire on faces, lanterns over tables, warm pools on planks. There is a bar with kegs and tankards, a hearth with a bench, a notice board, a streamer's corner and tables with live voxel miniatures. Press E at a chair and the camera glides down over the tabletop with no head or roof in the way. Leaving through the interior door returns you to the kingdom room at the exterior door, facing out.

**The games.** A handful of simple, fun, hand-made games, the kind you understand in one look:
- **Pong**, on the table in voxels: two paddles, one ball, first to 7. It is real time, with the server deciding and your own paddle moving the instant you move the mouse.
- **Stones**: drop stones into a wooden frame; four in a row wins.
- **Hearth Dice**: roll as often as you dare, a 1 loses what you rolled this turn, hold to bank it; first to 50 wins.
- **Arm Wrestling**: a timing duel; hit the beat and your arm pushes, and miss and it gives way.
- **The Axe Board**: throw at the board on the tavern wall; the aim sways with your breath, and you let go at the right moment.

Two walkers can play each other. A walker can play chat. The streamer sits at the corner table and plays chat, while chat votes its moves with a word in Twitch chat and watches it all on stream. A win leaves a small tally on your record, on the board by the bar, and the first win at each game leaves a keepsake with its date and place, ready for the bag and the shelf that are coming.

**The campfire at night.** At the edge of the wilds there is a clearing with a fire and a ring of logs. At night it is the warmest place in the kingdom: the crackle, embers rising into the stars, the light on the faces of whoever is sitting there. Twitch chat is there as voices around the fire: each chat line rises as a small lantern in the dark beyond the logs, with its words beside it (people who only chat are lights, never bodies, decision 13). Anyone walking can sit. When enough people sit, the fire can play **Dusk Court**: one of you is the wolf who snuffs a lantern each night, and the circle has until the lanterns run out to find them. It is short (about ten minutes), easy to follow on stream, and led by the streamer as Magistrate when live. Chat is the Gallery and points its suspicion with `!sus name`, and embers gather by the person it suspects.

The bar: every place and game is pleasant to watch without reading anything, fun on the first try, and works for two people on a quiet night as well as for a full room on stream.

- **Goals, not tasks.** A goal can take several sessions. Work in checkpoints: leave everything building and working, then write `next: GA10 <what is done / what remains>` as the last progress line. Tick a goal only when its done-when is truly met. Progress lines follow `<id>: <what + decisions + doubts> — <short sha> — <YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM>`. Commit on `lane/gather` only: `feat(gather): GA10 …`. Never push, never touch main.
- **Order.** GA10 first. It must be reviewed and checkpointed by Monday morning, so reach a clean, building checkpoint of it before anything else: the room and Pong first, then Stones and Hearth Dice. Then GA11, GA12 and GA13.
- **Your own servers and ports.** Run your own realm on a free port in 5170 to 5189 (check with `ss -ltn` first; avoid 5187), against your own database schema (`REALM_DATABASE_SCHEMA=realm_gather`, never the live schema), with its own founded directory under `/tmp/chatdom-gather/`. Found tobi4s_'s kingdom there the way the realm README and `packages/testing/kingdom-walk.mjs` do. Load `DATABASE_URL` with `--env-file` and never echo it. Write every port you use in progress. Start servers only for a test or a lease, and stop them afterwards. Apply `0016` only to your own schema.
- **Test multiplayer with real clients.** Every game is proven by at least two headless realm clients (the Colyseus SDK in Node, authenticated like real walkers through your own realm's session service) playing a whole match, plus a browser walker where the room itself is being checked. Inject latency for Pong. Keep these scripts in your scope and rerunnable.
- **Look at it on a real GPU.** Use `chatdom-pc-lease` (read its header): one lane at a time, at most 10 minutes. Inside the leased command, serve your build on 127.0.0.1:5192 and your realm on 5191, start your headless walkers against 5191 so the room is really playing, then call `chatdom-pc shot http://localhost:5192/<path> <out.png>` (and `chatdom-pc fps …`) with the full URL. `chatdom-pc shot` cannot type or click, so give the walk a dev parameter that signs a test walker into your own realm and seats it or places it at a spot. That parameter works only against a realm started with test sessions and is never reachable on the live site. If the tunnel is down (exit 3), take one short SwiftShader still under the shared capture lock (`packages/testing/capture-lock.mjs`, held under 5 minutes) and say so. SwiftShader frame times are never performance evidence. Open every still and check it against `chatdom/Quality.md` and `chatdom/Artstyle-Soul-Feel.md`; fix what looks cheap before you tick anything.
- **Server load.** The host has 12 cores shared with other live services. Run builds and tests under `nice -n 10`. Typecheck and test only the packages and files you touched (`pnpm exec vitest run <paths>`, the touched package's typecheck); never the full suite, never `pnpm verify`. No long recordings on the CPU. Measure the match room's own cost (tick time per table, bytes per client) and keep it small: an idle table costs nothing per tick.
- **Live services stay untouched.** Never start anything on 5190 or 5193 to 5197, never write the live realm's schema or founded directory, and never send to the live herald.
- **No SIMULATED labels.** Remove the chips in your scope: the campfire's `fixture.ts` and its surfaces, `/-/campfire`, and their READMEs and tests. Public surfaces show real people and real games or nothing. Dev benches may stage fixtures, unlabelled, and are never reachable as if they were real. An empty table is an empty table, never a pretend game.
- **Seams.** Read `SEAMS.md` before touching a place hook, a scene mount, the herald path or a realm core file. Never edit another lane's files. If a seam must change, write it in both lanes' `SEAMS.md` and add a `needs:` line. For integrator-owned code, write `needs:` with the patch spelled out and keep going on what doesn't depend on it. Until play's place hook and grand-world's mount land, build and prove everything on `/-/tavern` and `/-/campfire` against your own realm. The tavern page uses the same tavern room and scene as the portal destination; the campfire page uses the kingdom clearing mount. needs: play generic portal transfer and return; the older tavern place-mount request is retired.
- **Privacy.** Secret game state (roles, night taps, a die not yet shown) never enters public state, the stream view, logs, errors or evidence. Chat identities are kept only for counting and dropped when a window closes, except for those who earned a keepsake.
- **Evidence.** Stills, sheets and match logs go in `work/evidence/gather/` (PNGs under about 2 MB, clips under about 10 MB). End each session with what you verified, how, where the stills are and what you doubt.
- **Repo text.** No thread ids, and no names of development tools in commits or files.
- **Three strikes.** If the same failure happens three times, stop, write `blocked: <what you learned>` and move to another part of the goal.
