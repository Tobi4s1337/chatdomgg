## The part
You click into a kingdom and it is a game. The mouse is yours the whole time: it never wanders onto the second screen, a fast flick turns you as far as your hand moved, and Ctrl+W in fullscreen does nothing. Esc opens a carved ledger in the kit's wood, brass and parchment: settings you can actually tune (camera, first or third person, sensitivity, invert, field of view, volumes, label and bubble density, reduced motion, key bindings), your look, the build menu, the Forge, the way home and help. One click and you are walking again. V swaps first and third person smoothly.

The world is solid and honest underfoot. Your soles meet the drawn ground on flat streets, slopes, stairs and floors. The anvil, the forge and every civic fixture stop you. Grass sounds like grass, cobble like cobble, planks like planks, snow and sand and shallows like themselves, and the walkers around you have quiet steps of their own. The only bodies in the streets are people walking right now; everyone chatting from home is their lit window and a bubble that rises from it. Bubbles sit in the world like they belong there: steady through every camera move, eased in and out, faded behind walls, nearest first, readable. A small painted minimap shows the places, your homes and your friends, never strangers, and a trail of lanterns walks you home.

And you look like you. Your look is saved to your Twitch identity on the server, so a second browser, the stream, your card and the panel all show the same person. The builder is made with love: beautiful type, item cards that tell each piece's story, a close-up of what you wear, a reveal for every unlock, earned pieces you can read, and many new pieces in the accepted style, some of them funny. On your first walk a mirror asks for three quick picks; at home a wardrobe lets you change and walk out wearing it. Mirror mode is gone from building, and the build menu is a clear carved rack with your real name and your pieces one tap away.

- **V means first or third person.** Mouse look is always on, so the old V (opt-in mouse look) is gone. F still uses the nearest highlighted thing, and the centre of the screen is the pick point.
- **No dead controls.** A ledger page or setting without a real provider is not shown.

## How to work
- **One goal per worker, in order.** Read this PRD, `SEAMS.md`, `progress.txt` and the context rows before you start, and claim your goal with a `claim:` progress line. Never pick from Backlog. Ideas go to Backlog as one line each.
- **Who touches what** (the two goals never edit the same file):
  - PL10: `apps/web/src/walk/**`, `apps/web/src/play/**` (except one import line per provider in `providers.ts`), the `kingdom/` walk files listed in Scope, `citizens/footsteps.ts` and the gait, the new step files in `packages/audio`, and the mount lines in `kingdom/index.tsx`.
  - PL11: `apps/realm/src/look/**` and its migration, the authenticator and realm `main.ts` lines, `packages/shared/src/look-store.ts`, `apps/web/src/citizens/**` except `footsteps.ts`, `apps/web/src/avatar-builder/**`, `packages/content/src/avatars/**`, `packages/content/src/avatar-style/**`, `apps/web/src/build/**`, `apps/web/src/build-tools/**`, plus one line in `play/providers.ts`.
  - PL12 takes over `apps/web/src/play/**` once PL10 is ticked.
  - If you need a file the other goal owns, write `ask: <goal> <exact change>` in progress and keep going on something else.
- **Checkpoints, not one big bang.** A goal takes many sessions. End every session with everything building and working, and a line `next: <id> <what is done / what remains>`. When a part is ready for review, write `checkpoint: <id> <what is in it, how it was checked, evidence paths>`. A reviewer reads checkpoints and commits them; the integrator merges them continuously from tonight.
- **Never run git.** No commit, add, stash, checkout, merge, rebase or reset. A reviewer commits. Progress lines carry no commit hash: `<id>: <what you did + decisions + doubts> — <YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM>`.
- **Tick a goal only when its whole Done-when is met.** Then add `LANE COMPLETE` as the last line once every goal is ticked.
- **Look at it on a real GPU.** Use `chatdom-pc-lease` (read its header): one lane at a time, at most 10 minutes. Inside the command, serve your own build on 127.0.0.1:5192 (5191 for your own realm), wait for it, and shoot `http://localhost:5192/...` with the `pc shot` command in the same folder at the sizes in Done-when. Never use 5190. If the lease returns 3 (the tunnel is down), take one short SwiftShader still under the shared capture lock (hold it under 5 minutes) and note that a real-GPU still is still owed. SwiftShader frame times are never performance evidence. Look at every still yourself against `chatdom/Quality.md` and fix what looks cheap.
- **The host is shared.** Typecheck and test only what you touched (for example `nice -n 10 pnpm --filter @chatdom/web exec vitest run src/walk src/play`, `nice -n 10 pnpm --filter @chatdom/realm exec vitest run src/look`). Never run the full suite or `pnpm verify`. Keep builds niced, no long CPU recordings, and run your own servers only during a lease or a test.
- **Secrets:** never print or read `~/.config/chatdom/*.env`, the repo `.env` or other credential stores; load them with `--env-file` or `source` without echoing.
- **No SIMULATED labels** anywhere in play's scope. Public surfaces show real data or nothing. Fixtures live only on dev benches, unlabelled, and are never reachable as if real.
- **Seams.** Consume the ground and collision query exactly as `SEAMS.md` says. Never edit land generation, `scene.ts` or the founded-scene assembly; ask grand-world with an exact `needs:` (the patch written out) and keep going. Provide the HUD slots exactly as `SEAMS.md` says. Changing a seam means writing the change in both lanes' `SEAMS.md` through the integrator (a `needs:` line), never a silent change.
- **New dependencies** only if truly needed, and say so in progress (the lockfile merges are painful). None should be needed here.
- **Three strikes.** If the same failure happens three times, write `blocked: <what you learned>` and move to another part of your goal.
- **Repo text:** plain English, no thread ids, no tool or model names.
