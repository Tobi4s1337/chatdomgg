# Monarch

The streamer's side of Chatdom. Internal. Started 2026-10-29.

This file sets out:
- who the monarch is
- the rules for everything a streamer touches
- how a streamer finds Chatdom and founds a kingdom, live
- how the kingdom sits in a stream, night after night
- the throne: the monarch's powers, used with one hand
- what the channel's moderators do
- how all of it grows over months, and how it ends

It is derived from `ChatDom.md`, above all §4 (the monarch feels proud), principle 7 (the kingdom tells its own story)
and §9 (never a chore for the streamer). It refers to its siblings rather than repeating them:
- `Twitch.md` owns what each Twitch event and each royal power that writes to Twitch does: decrees as polls, Crown vs
  Crowd, the Raid Gate, knighting and VIP, the kingdom rewards and the communal pool, the Herald's lines and the panel.
- `World-Tree.md` owns the beings, things, places and acts, including the castle, the plaza and the act of ruling.
- `Atlas.md` owns the map, the raid finder, waiting land and settlers' tents.
- `Safety.md` owns the rules for what people make, the judges, the stream pass, Wardens and the Watch's review. This
  file owns the console that shows them.

This file owns the monarch's experience of all of those. When it disagrees with `ChatDom.md`, `ChatDom.md` wins.
Otherwise it changes on purpose, with a line in the change log. The founder's decisions of 2026-10-29 are in §13.

Streamers decide whether Chatdom reaches a stream at all. So every line here answers one test: would a busy streamer,
mid-game, with one hand on the mouse, be glad this exists?

## 1. The monarch's side in one page

**The promise to a streamer.** Connect your channel in about two minutes. On your next stream, press one button and
your kingdom is founded live from your channel, with your chat naming it and watching it rise. After that it tells its
own story on your stream, needs nothing from you to keep going, and gives you something to be proud of every night.

**How Chatdom sits in a stream.** The kingdom is the stream's home, not a replacement for its content. It has four
places, and the streamer chooses how many to use:

1. **The seams.** Every stream has a starting scene, breaks and an ending, and most of them are a countdown, a still
   image or a loop. The kingdom becomes those scenes: chat arriving as lights at the start, the night so far in a
   break, the Raid Gate and the Ballad at the end. This is the first thing we ask for, because it costs the streamer
   nothing.
2. **The window.** A small carved frame in a corner, or a strip along the bottom edge, over any game. Nearly silent,
   light on the PC, cutting to the night's moments. This is what we hope most streamers keep on.
3. **The stage.** The kingdom fills the screen with the streamer's camera over it: Just Chatting, game nights, the
   campfire, a royal audience, building the castle, the dragon, and the founding itself.
4. **Nowhere on screen.** The kingdom still works for chat and walkers, and still sings the Ballad.

The kingdom takes more of the screen only on the streamer's press, or for moments they chose to let open by themselves
(founder decision). It is a layer first. Kingdom nights, where it is the evening's content, are supported in the
streamer's usual category (founder decision).

The evidence agrees. Formats that asked to be the main content died: Twitch Sings closed, and Squad Stream reached
under 1% of Partner streams ([Twitch blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2023/12/13/retiring-squad-stream/)). Tools that
sat beside the content lasted: Stream Avatars has had about a thousand copies running at any moment for five years,
mostly as a strip along the bottom ([Steam Charts](https://steamcharts.com/app/665300)), and Marbles on Stream is used
to start a stream or fill a queue ([its developer](https://steamcommunity.com/app/1170970/discussions/0/1743386132101466701/)).

## 2. Who the monarch is

The monarch is the person whose Twitch channel the kingdom belongs to. One person can be the monarch of their own
kingdom and a citizen with a home in other kingdoms (founder decision). At home they have the castle and the throne.
Abroad, the crown stays home (`World-Tree.md` rule 9, `Twitch.md` §12). Only the monarch presses royal acts (founder
decision).

Size bands follow `Moments.md` §1 until the founder settles one bracket table.

| Monarch | What they want most | What they fear | Where the kingdom sits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tiny** (under 10 viewers, most of Twitch) | To look alive, to know everyone, to be found | Looking empty; one more thing to set up | The seams, the window, and often a kingdom night. They are their own Watch |
| **Small to mid** (10 to 500) | Rituals that bring regulars back; a flex | Spam, more moderation, lost focus | The seams, the window, a weekly game night. The design target |
| **Large** (500 to 10,000) | Spectacle that matches the crowd; collabs | One bad thing on stream becoming a clip; hate raids | The seams and segments, big moments on the stage. A mod team, often a producer |
| **Capital** (10,000 and more) | A city of their people | Embarrassment at scale; anything they cannot control live | Segments and big moments. Rarely the window: their screen is already designed |

| Kind of stream | What changes |
|---|---|
| **Variety** | The community follows the person, not the game, and the kingdom is the one constant. Streamers "pick one overlay and stick to it" ([Twitch developer forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/21201/14)). The weather follows the category |
| **One competitive game** | Nothing asks for attention during a match. Busy (§6.4) keeps the kingdom quiet; queues, death screens and breaks are its moments |
| **Just Chatting and IRL** | The calm stage can be the backdrop all evening. Audiences, citizen cards and the campfire are natural talk. IRL streamers use the phone throne |
| **Creative** | The castle, the plaza and build mode are content. A castle-building evening is a kingdom night of its own |

**The moderators** are the Watch (§8). A tiny channel often has none, and the streamer is the Watch with Chatdom's
judges behind them. A mid channel has 2 to 5 volunteer mods, often friends. A large one has a team and a lead
moderator. Most channels already run a bot (Nightbot, StreamElements, Fossabot, Streamer.bot), and the Herald lives
beside it without collisions.

## 3. Principles

Each principle comes with **why** and **so**, as in `ChatDom.md`.

**1. The kingdom works when the monarch ignores it.**
- **Why:** principle 7. Stream Raiders stranded viewers when captains forgot to start battles, and shut down in March
  2025 (`docs/08-depth-viewer-games.md` §2).
- **So:**
  - Every system runs on its own clock with a fallback. Nothing a citizen does waits on a royal press, except an
    honour.
  - Big moments never wait for the monarch either: they play on Twitch's clock (founder decision), and the stage
    replays what the streamer missed at the next break (§6.4).
  - A night with no royal press is still a good night. A suggestion expires quietly and never returns as a debt.

**2. One hand, one gesture, and only the monarch's.**
- **Why:** principle 7. Streamers play with both hands busy, and 64% stream on one monitor
  ([Streamlabs](https://streamlabs.com/content-hub/post/streamlabs-game-overlay-for-single-monitors)). Royal acts mean
  something because the monarch chose them (founder decision: only the streamer presses royal acts).
- **So:**
  - Every royal act is one press on the throne, one tap on a phone, one Stream Deck key or one chat line from the
    broadcaster's own account.
  - No act needs a settings page, a text box, a drag or two hands. Choices come as at most three named options.
  - Nobody presses a royal act for the monarch: not a mod, an editor or a producer. Mods keep the Watch's tools and
    the safety keys, and Wardens decide holds (§8).
  - A web page cannot hear the keyboard while a game has focus, so no act depends on a keyboard shortcut.

**3. The kingdom proposes; the monarch nods.**
- **Why:** the kingdom remembers everything and the streamer cannot. With nobody to delegate to, timing is everything.
- **So:**
  - The kingdom offers one suggestion at a time, at a natural pause: a break, a queue, the end of a game. "7 citizens
    are ready for knighthood", "HexHeroes is live and you have a road to them".
  - Each has two answers, yes and not tonight. Ignoring it means not tonight.
  - A few acts can run by a standing choice the monarch made once, such as a saved game playlist that advances by
    itself. Only a handful of suggestions arrive a night.

**4. The stream belongs to the streamer.**
- **Why:** streamers drop tools that pull focus or flood chat. Bots post about 300 times more often than people
  ([Seering and others, 2018](https://joseph.seering.org/papers/Seering_etal_2018_Social_Chatbots.pdf)), and a game
  that posted as the streamer got large streamers chat-banned ([Twitch forum](https://discuss.dev.twitch.com/t/9888)).
- **So:**
  - **Screen:** the kingdom never covers the camera or the game's interface. It takes more of the screen only on a
    press, or for moments the monarch chose to let open by themselves, each with a 5-second "not now".
  - **Sound:** quiet by default, on its own OBS fader.
  - **Chat:** the Herald speaks rarely (founder decision; `Twitch.md` §13). No timers, no adverts.
  - Nothing goes to Twitch in the streamer's name without a preview (`Tech.md` §7.4). The kingdom never makes clips
    or videos of its own (founder decision).

**5. The streamer is never embarrassed on stream.**
- **Why:** Twitch holds streamers responsible for what appears on their stream, and protects those who act in good
  faith with the tools they have ([Community Guidelines](https://safety.twitch.tv/s/article/Community-Guidelines)).
  Streamers have been banned for a viewer's text-to-speech slur that played while they were away
  ([Daily Dot](https://www.dailydot.com/upstream/twitch-streamer-racist-text-to-speech/)).
- **So:**
  - Nothing reaches the stream view that has not passed its checks (`Safety.md` §5.3), and the director never shows a
    secret or a sanction (`Tech.md` §6.8).
  - The streamer chooses the stream pass, a human look before anything citizens made airs, when they connect. It is
    on by default above 1,000 viewers (founder decision).
  - A refusal may air only as a nameless comic beat that never shows or hints at the words (founder decision,
    `Safety.md` §5.3).
  - The stream view never shows a viewer count, or any number that makes a small stream look empty. It shows lights,
    names and carved meters.
  - Three safety keys sit on every form of the throne (§7.4). The founding ceremony airs only what has passed (§5.2).

**6. Never felt on the streamer's PC.**
- **Why:** Quality rule 1. Animated browser sources are reported using 20 to 60% of a CPU core
  ([OBS forum](https://obsproject.com/forum/threads/browser-source-very-slow-and-cpu-heavy.133655/)), and streamers
  remove what slows them down.
- **So:**
  - The stream view keeps to about 4 ms of GPU a frame, stops drawing when hidden, and lowers its internal resolution
    while the PC is busy (founder decision; `Tech.md` §8.5).
  - The throne shows the stream view's cost plainly, and draws no 3D world of its own.

**7. Two minutes to be ready; the kingdom is born live.**
- **Why:** the commonest reason Twitch's own alerts fail is that the browser source never reached OBS
  ([Twitch alerts FAQ](https://help.twitch.tv/s/article/alerts-by-twitch-faq)). And the founder chose a live founding,
  with no private draft, so the birth of the kingdom is the community's moment.
- **So:**
  - Connecting asks for three things: consent, where the kingdom sits on screen, and the stream pass (preset by size).
    It takes about two minutes and shows a test on stream at once (§6.7).
  - Nothing of the kingdom is drafted or shown before the founding ceremony. The ceremony is built to be safe live:
    every step airs only what has passed, every wait is filled with something true, and every failure has a graceful
    path (§5.2).
  - Everything else is offered later, one thing at a time.

**8. The same crown for every size.**
- **Why:** principles 1 and 9. Marbles on Stream was hurt by a paid editor (`docs/08-depth-viewer-games.md` §2), and
  Crowd Control was resented for moving the setting small channels need behind its paid plan
  ([Crowd Control](https://crowdcontrol.medium.com/important-announcement-crowd-control-pro-price-increase-904644b9c4b1)).
- **So:**
  - Genesis, the stream view, the director and every royal power are the same at 5 viewers and at 50,000.
  - No power is gated on Affiliate status. Where Twitch refuses, the kingdom falls back to a chat vote
    (`Twitch.md` §14).
  - Nothing a streamer needs to rule is ever paid for. Chatdom earns first from its share of Bits in the panel
    (founder decision, `Twitch.md` §18).

**9. The crown honours; it never takes.**
- **Why:** principle 15 says nothing a person does harms another's home or standing, and the monarch is a person.
- **So:**
  - The monarch can raise people up: knight, bless, summon, name, crown a game, pick a home.
  - The monarch cannot touch anyone's home, things, light, rank or history. Behaviour is dealt with on Twitch, and the
    world follows.
  - Royal favour is always public, with the monarch's name and the date. An honour can be released with a reason, but
    what it earned stays in the history.

**10. The mods decide; Chatdom does the first pass.**
- **Why:** principle 15 makes the channel's mods the authority. They are volunteers, and a queue they must clear is a
  chore for them and for the streamer.
- **So:**
  - Mods' powers in the world follow their Twitch role, live. Wardens, if the monarch appoints them, decide holds only
    (founder decision).
  - The judges do the first pass (`Safety.md` §4). The Watch sees only what the judges could not decide, and nothing
    waits on them. Holds nobody decided stay private, and the monarch sees the count at go-live (founder decision).
  - A kingdom with no mods is still safe. The streamer is never the moderator of last resort.

**11. The flex is what the community made.**
- **Why:** `ChatDom.md` §4 ("look what my community built") and principle 14.
- **So:**
  - Everything a monarch shows off is the citizens' work: streets, the wonder, the knights' roll, roads, the crown.
  - Comparisons stay inside size leagues, never by viewers or money. Every moment on stream names the people in it.

**12. A break is never a debt.**
- **Why:** principle 5. Streamers take holidays, change games, burn out and stop.
- **So:**
  - The monarch has no streaks, no daily tasks and no "your kingdom misses you" messages.
  - A resting kingdom loses nothing (§10). Coming back is a homecoming, not a list of what went wrong.

## 4. Finding Chatdom

A streamer arrives only by choosing to. Chatdom never reads a chat that has not connected (`Tech.md` §2, principle 9),
so every path ends with the streamer pressing "Found your kingdom" themselves.

| Path | What the streamer sees | What we do, and never do |
|---|---|---|
| **Their viewers** | Tents on their waiting land, pitched by signed-in viewers; the card shows the count in bands from 20 (founder decision, `Atlas.md`) | The streamer learns the exact number only at founding, when it is read aloud. We never message the streamer or their chat |
| **A raid** | A Chatdom kingdom raids them, and the raiders talk about the road | The road ends at their waiting land (`Atlas.md` §7.6). Nothing is posted in their chat |
| **The Atlas** | Their name on the map of Twitch, near the channels they raid | The first Twitch Atlas went viral and streamers reacted on stream (`docs/11`). Any streamer can hide their land (`Atlas.md` §9) |
| **Another streamer** | A friend's kingdom on stream: the dragon, the procession, a Ballad | Nothing extra. The stream view carries no Chatdom watermark, only the kingdom's own crest |
| **A viewer's clip** | A clip a viewer made of a friend's kingdom, shared anywhere | Nothing extra. Chatdom makes no clips, recaps or videos of its own (founder decision) |

We never send cold messages, put a bot in a channel that has not connected, fake activity on anyone's land or sell
places on the map. A public tent count is gentle pressure on a streamer who never asked; it starts at 20 and is shown
only in bands, and any streamer can hide their land.

## 5. Founding the kingdom

### 5.1 Connecting: about two minutes, before any stream

1. **Found your kingdom,** on chatdom.gg or on their land on the Atlas. Twitch asks for tier 1 only: six scopes
   (`Tech.md` §7.2). Chatdom gathers the channel's data now (emotes, colours, follow dates, category), so nothing on
   stream waits for Twitch later. Codex does not run yet.
2. **The stream pass.** One switch, preset by size: on above 1,000 average viewers, off below (founder decision,
   `Safety.md` §5.8).
3. **Place it on screen.** Pictures of the seams, the window and the stage, each with a link to drag into OBS (§6.8).
4. **Ring the bell.** One button rings the bell on the stream view so the streamer sees it working in OBS. Until the
   founding, the stream view shows the waiting land in pencil, in the Atlas's look, with the settlers' tents and a
   light for each chatter: a teaser that is already true.

**Offered later, one at a time, never while connecting:**
- tier 2, "every Twitch moment", after the founding; tier 3 scopes the first time each power is used
- making the Herald a mod, after the founding stream, with one line to paste in chat. It raises the Herald's chat
  budget and lets the crier make announcements (`Twitch.md` §13). The kingdom works without it, because the Chat Bot
  badge needs only the streamer's grant ([Twitch chat docs](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/chat/))
- the Chatdom panel under the video, after the founding stream (`Twitch.md` §16). Twitch needs two steps for it,
  install and activate

### 5.2 The founding ceremony, live

Genesis runs only live, on stream, at the monarch's press, with no private draft (founder decision). So the ceremony
is designed as a show that is safe live. It lasts about 3 to 4 minutes on the stage. Codex's 1 to 3 minutes of work
(`Tech.md` §11.2) happen behind steps that show only things that are already true.

| Step | About | On stream | Behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The call** | 0:00 | The monarch presses Found (the throne, or `!found`). The stage opens on the map of Twitch, and the camera flies to their waiting land | Genesis starts. It writes names first |
| **2. The settlers** | 0:20 | The tents are counted aloud, and the exact number is revealed for the first time: "47 of you were already here". The first settlers' names hang on their tents | Codex drafts names |
| **3. The gathering** | 0:40 | The channel's emotes fly in as wax seals, the channel's colours are mixed in an ink pot, the crest is drawn. All of it comes from data, already checked | Codex writes the charter: landscape, landmarks, layout, with its top three landscapes ranked |
| **4. The naming** | 1:20 | Three names that passed the name judges appear. Chat votes for 45 seconds in chat, so founding needs no extra scope. With fewer than 5 voters, the monarch picks from chat's favourite | The generator builds the land from the charter; beauty checks run (Quality rule 17) |
| **5. The drawing** | 2:15 | The cartographer inks the map: coast, river, the castle's hill, the tenure quarters, each with a short reason ("a mountain, because this channel climbs") | Checks finish on the layout's postcard angles |
| **6. Keep or redraw** | 2:45 | The monarch sees the inked map with chat. One press keeps it. Redraw crumples the parchment and the cartographer draws again, from the charter's next landscape, in about 20 seconds. At most two redraws | The next layout, from the same charter, with no new Codex run |
| **7. The rise** | 3:00 | 15 seconds: the ink rises into voxels, the castle assembles, the emote statues extrude, the beacon fires. The director keeps stills for the first Chronicle | The kingdom is saved |
| **8. The first lights** | After | Homes appear in their tenure quarters as chatters speak: a four-year follower lands in the oldest quarter, by the castle (`World-Tree.md`) | An ordinary night begins |

**When something goes wrong:**
- **Nothing unchecked airs.** Names, statues and the layout pass their checks before they appear. An emote the judges
  find awkward as a 3 m statue is set aside quietly, for the monarch to raise later (`Safety.md` §6, case 6).
- **Fewer than three good names** are topped up with names built from the channel's name, which are checked the same
  way.
- **Codex is slow.** The gathering stretches with more true beats: more emotes, the first-day followers' names read
  from the oldest quarter's gate. At 4 minutes the ceremony takes a fallback charter built without Codex from the
  category and colours, and carries on without a visible stall. There is never a loading sign.
- **Codex is down.** The fallback charter founds the kingdom on time.
- **The stream drops mid-ceremony.** Nothing is half-founded. The next press starts the ceremony again from the call.
- **A draw the monarch dislikes** is what Redraw is for, live, with chat watching.
- **Afterwards,** names, crest, colours and statues can change at any time, each with a small ceremony. The land holds,
  because homes stand on it and nobody moves (`World-Tree.md`). It grows outward instead.

The real risk of a live-only founding is that the kingdom's first look is public, so a dull draw is seen by everyone.
Redraw is the safety valve, and the beauty checks are the floor.

**When to found.** The throne suggests founding from the stage once chat has gathered, and never during a match. A
streamer can found on any stream after connecting. Chatdom makes no founding video; viewers may clip it on Twitch, and
the director keeps stills (founder decision).

### 5.3 Chosen, given and fixed

| Part | Chosen | Given by default | Later |
|---|---|---|---|
| Name and motto | By chat's vote from three, live; the monarch picks under 5 voters | The first suggestion | A renaming ceremony on stream |
| Crest and colours | Optional | From the profile image and name colour | Any time |
| Plaza statues | Which emotes stand there (`Safety.md` §5.8) | Sub emotes first | Any time; new emotes get an unveiling |
| Landscape and landmarks | Keep or redraw, live, up to twice | The charter's first landscape, from the category | Fixed after the rise |
| Size | No | From followers, subs and average viewers | Grows from activity alone |
| Style kit | No | From colours, emotes and landscape | Fixed: it is the kingdom's culture |
| Where it sits on stream | Yes | The seams | Any time |
| The stream pass | Yes, when connecting | On above 1,000 average viewers | Any time |
| The Herald in chat | Silent, rare or herald | Rare | Any time |
| Making modes and Wardens | Optional (`Safety.md` §5.8) | No Wardens | Any time |
| Voice at the campfire and throne room | Off until first used | Off | Any time |

- **Every channel gets a beautiful kingdom.** No emotes and a default avatar still give a lovely hamlet (Quality
  rule 17).
- **History is there on night one.** Genesis lays out the tenure quarters by follow year, with sub months counted as
  `World-Tree.md` sets out, so nobody waits to earn their past.
- **Settlers are kept.** Their tents become homes at the edge of their quarter, and the first Chronicle names them.

### 5.4 The rest of the founding stream

The founding stream teaches by doing, never by a manual:
1. After the rise, the content writes itself. The streamer reads names off the screen as homes appear ("cozy_otter,
   you've followed since 2022, you live by the castle").
2. If the streamer goes back to a game, the window, if placed, cuts to the night's moments.
3. One suggestion, later: a knighting for the longest follower present.
4. At the end, the Raid Gate with friends who are live draws the first road on the Atlas. Then the first Ballad, from
   which the monarch may strike lines before it is pinned.
5. Afterwards, the first Chronicle, with the director's stills of the rise.

Beyond the press that founds it, the first night asks for at most two presses, and both are optional.

## 6. How Chatdom sits in a stream

This is the question that decides whether a kingdom stays on a stream for a week or for years.

### 6.1 Every stream has seams

Most streams share one shape: a starting scene while chat gathers (often 5 to 15 minutes), the main content, breaks
(BRB, ads, queues, a change of game) and an ending. Streamers already put a full-screen scene in each seam, and nothing
happens in most of them. The seams are when chat is the content, and the kingdom is chat made visible. So the seams
are the kingdom's first home.

### 6.2 Four places, one kingdom

| Place | What it looks like | When | Sound |
|---|---|---|---|
| **The seams** (the first ask) | The stage, used as starting, break and ending scenes | Going live, BRB, between games, the raid out | Music and ambience, under the microphone |
| **The window** | A carved frame in a corner, about a quarter of the width, or a strip along the bottom | During play | Nearly silent: a bell or a swell for a big moment |
| **The stage** | The kingdom fills the screen, the camera over it | Kingdom nights, game nights, campfire, audiences, building, big moments, the founding | The full broadcast mix |
| **Nowhere** | Nothing | Any time | None |

**The seams.**
- **Starting:** the sky spins to dusk, the beacon catches, and each arriving chatter lights a window. Chat replaces the
  countdown.
- **Breaks:** the director tours the night so far, and replays any big moment the streamer missed while Busy (§6.4).
  The communal pool's progress shows here, as the town hall or tavern rising in scaffold and a carved meter, never a
  number of points (founder decision; `Twitch.md` §8). During a Twitch ad break the kingdom holds its breath
  (`Twitch.md` §7), because viewers are watching ads.
- **Ending:** the Raid Gate, the procession, and the Ballad over the night town with everyone whose light burned
  tonight. It replaces the end-credits screen.

**The window.**
- **Two shapes.** The corner frame suits games with an empty corner. The strip suits games with a free bottom edge,
  where streamers have kept Stream Avatars for years. In the strip the camera walks one street at a time, and lights
  come on as chatters arrive.
- **Made for small:** closer shots, slower cuts, bigger subjects. A wide shot of a whole town is noise at a quarter of
  the screen. No text except a name at a climax.
- **Alive at 5 viewers:** the keepers of places, chimney smoke, birds, weather (Quality rule 15, `World-Tree.md` §3).
- **It can open.** One press swings its shutters wide and the kingdom fills the screen for a big moment, then closes.

**The stage** has two moods. Calm is a backdrop behind a talking streamer: slow drifts, no hard cuts. Show is for
segments and big moments, with cuts on beats. The streamer marks where their camera and chat box sit, once, and the
director keeps subjects and text out of those areas.

**Nowhere** is the smallest yes, and enough to start: homes, lights, the tavern and the Ballad, with nothing on screen.

### 6.3 By kind of stream

| Stream | The seams | The window | The stage |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tiny | Yes | Yes | Often: a weekly kingdom night, building together, games with chat |
| Variety | Yes | Yes, the constant across games | Between games; game nights |
| One competitive game | Yes, and queues | Calm during matches (Busy) | Breaks only |
| Just Chatting and IRL | Yes | Rarely | The calm stage all evening; show mood for segments |
| Large and capital | Yes | Rarely | Segments and big moments |

A kingdom night (building the castle or the plaza together, a game night, the campfire, a festival) runs in the
streamer's usual category. Chatdom does not seek a category of its own (founder decision).

### 6.4 Big moments, Show and Busy

The dragon, a raid arriving, a crowning, a wonder unveiled or a pool level completing deserve the whole screen. They
come on Twitch's clock, and the streamer may be mid-fight.

- **Show.** When a big moment is ready, the throne shows one button. It opens the window to the stage.
- **Moments that open by themselves.** In Settings the monarch can tick moments that may open the stage without a
  press, for example the dragon or a raid arriving. The throne then gives 5 seconds of "not now" before it opens
  (founder decision).
- **Busy** is one switch on the throne, the phone and the Stream Deck. While it is on, the throne makes no suggestions,
  nothing opens the stage by itself, and the window keeps calm framing. Busy can come on by itself for a category
  ("whenever I play Valorant"), and always lifts when the stream ends.
- **A climax never waits** (founder decision). It plays on Twitch's clock for chat and walkers, and the window shows it
  small if it is placed.
- **Earlier tonight.** At the next break, the stage opens with a replay of what the streamer missed, about 20
  seconds, with the names, clearly labelled as a replay. The streamer can react and thank people, and watchers who
  missed it see it too.

The real risk of never waiting is that a streamer's live reaction is the best moment on stream, and a replay is the
second best.

### 6.5 The kingdom stays out of the game

Chatdom never reaches into the game the streamer is playing. It is not Crowd Control. The kingdom reads only the
category from Twitch, for its weather. Setup stays one link, and a ranked match can never be ruined by the kingdom.

### 6.6 Walkers during a live stream

chatdom.gg shows no Twitch player (founder decision). So that walking does not pull viewers off the stream:
- **Watch alongside** opens Twitch's own pop-out player in its own small window, and the walking page lays itself out
  to sit beside it. On phones, the Twitch app's picture-in-picture does the same where the phone supports it.
- **The world points to the show.** A soft beam rises over whatever the director is filming, so walkers can go where
  the stream is looking and be part of the shot.
- **Royal moments call walkers to the stage's place.** A knighting or an audience lights the throne room's windows.

The real risk is that some walkers mute or close the stream. Measure it on the test channels.

### 6.7 Sound

- The stream view plays its own broadcast mix: music, ambience, spectacle and the Voice of the Realm, never UI clicks
  or footsteps (`Tech.md` §8.7). It starts well under the microphone.
- The window plays no music, so it never fights the game. On the stage, music grows with the people present.
- All music is original and cleared for VODs. Twitch Sings had to delete every Sings VOD when it closed
  ([Twitch blog](https://blog.twitch.tv/en/2020/09/04/twitch-sings-closes-january-1/)).
- The throne has three faders: music, spectacle and voices. Campfire voices play only on the stage, while the
  streamer is seated (`Tech.md` §10.5).

### 6.8 Setting it up in OBS

- **One dragged link per place.** OBS turns a link dragged onto it into a browser source with the name and size the
  link carries ([OBS drag-and-drop](https://obsproject.com/tools/browser-drag-and-drop)). The throne offers a stage
  link (1920 by 1080) and a window link in either shape. Other apps take the same link by paste.
- **The seams reuse the stage source** in the starting, BRB and ending scenes.
- **Two OBS settings,** ticked once: "Control audio via OBS" and a custom frame rate of 60. The throne checks both
  live and says plainly if one is off.
- **The throne as a dock,** beside chat.
- **The secret link** keeps its token after the `#`, and rotates in one press if it shows on stream (`Tech.md` §8.8).
- **The stream view asks OBS for nothing** beyond whether it is visible. Moments that open by themselves do so inside
  the kingdom's own source. Switching the streamer's scenes would need OBS's "advanced access" permission
  ([obs-browser](https://github.com/obsproject/obs-browser/blob/master/README.md)), which is asked for only if the
  monarch wants a scene switch rather than the window opening.

### 6.9 Chat

- The kingdom tells its story on stream, not in chat (founder decision). The streamer sets the Herald to silent, rare
  (default) or herald. Its lines are in `Twitch.md` §13.
- No timers and no adverts. `!chatdom` answers with the link.
- Chat words never clash with tools the channel already runs. Marbles on Stream answers `!play`, so the kingdom never
  uses it, and the streamer can rename or switch off any of its words.

## 7. The throne

### 7.1 Four forms, one page

The throne is the monarch's console at `chatdom.gg/<login>/throne` (`Tech.md` §8.8). It answers only to the
broadcaster's own Twitch account. Mods who open it see the Watch drawer and the safety keys, nothing royal.

| Form | Best for |
|---|---|
| **The dock,** an OBS custom browser dock beside chat | One monitor; quick presses between moments |
| **The second screen,** a browser tab | The fullest view |
| **The phone,** upright with large buttons, installable | One hand mid-game; IRL; away from the desk |
| **The Stream Deck,** one key per act | Streamers who run everything from keys, pressed without looking |

Chat is a fifth way: the broadcaster can type any royal act as one line (`!found`, `!gate`, `!knight cozy_otter`,
`!busy`). Royal acts are public anyway. The monarch can also walk the kingdom and act in it, as a choice for the stage.

### 7.2 The front

The front holds only what a monarch may need in the next ten seconds:
- **Busy**
- **the three safety keys** (§7.4)
- **one suggestion,** with yes and not tonight
- **Show,** only when a big moment is ready
- **the night so far:** "cozy_otter forged a dragon chimney · 12 new homes · the Watch: all quiet"
- **at go-live only,** the count of holds that waited since last stream, with one press to open them (founder
  decision)
- **health:** the stream view running, its frame rate and cost, sound flowing

Everything else is one press away in drawers: Decrees, The gate, Honours, The tavern, The kingdom, The Watch and
Settings. Settings hold where the kingdom sits, the faders, Busy by category, the moments that may open by themselves,
the Herald, chat words, `Safety.md` §5.8's controls (making modes, the stream pass, the kingdom's words, Wardens,
statues), the kingdom rewards and the communal pool, resting and leaving. The throne is carved from the same kit as
every surface (Quality rule 14) and reads at a glance in a narrow dock.

### 7.3 The powers

What each power does in the world is `World-Tree.md` §6. What writes to Twitch, and Twitch's limits, is `Twitch.md`
§13. This is how the monarch uses them, always with their own hand.

| Power | What the monarch does | Presses | What happens by itself | Suggested? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Found** | Starts the founding ceremony, once | 1, then keep or redraw | The whole ceremony (§5.2) | Yes, once chat has gathered |
| **Decree** | Picks one of three proposals, sees the preview, issues it as a poll | 2 | The winner is built or happens | Yes: what to build, a festival's theme, street names |
| **Crown vs Crowd** | Challenges chat at a tavern table | 1 | The prediction, the odds board, the result | On game nights |
| **The Raid Gate** | Picks a live channel from the raid finder | 2 | The march, the road, the raid | Yes: friends with roads first, smaller channels first (`Atlas.md` §7.6) |
| **Knighting** | Dubs one citizen, or "Dub all" who are ready | 1 | Ceremony, serial, the roll of knights. VIP only by an extra click (`Twitch.md` §13) | Yes: by tenure and deeds |
| **Royal audience** | Opens a sign-up; drawn citizens come to the dais | 1, then 1 each | The draw, by tenure and not drawn lately (`docs/08-depth-social.md` §3.14) | On Just Chatting nights |
| **Choose the wonder** | Picks the next wonder's design from three | 1 | Citizens build it into that design (`World-Tree.md` §5) | At a season's start |
| **Crown a game** | Crowns a prompted game for the kingdom | 1 | The author's name on it; the crown lane (`Tech.md` §10.3) | Yes: the most played |
| **Start a game** | Starts a table from the saved playlist | 1, or a playlist set to advance by itself | Seats, rounds, payouts (`docs/08-depth-viewer-games.md` §3.6) | On game night |
| **King's Pick** | Crowns one home a stream | 1, or `!pick name` | Pennant and crowning shot | When chat keeps naming one |
| **Festival** | Calls one festival a stream | 1 | Lights, music, a bonus inside the daily caps | Season festivals run themselves |
| **The forge roars** | Decrees 15 minutes of roaring forge | 1 | Two extra anvils light, so more can forge at once. No coals: coals come only from showing up (founder decision, `Safety.md` §3.2) | After a quiet spell at the forge |
| **The Ballad** | Strikes a line before it is pinned | 1, or nothing | Pinned by itself if nobody strikes | After every stream |
| **Campfire** | Sits as Magistrate | 1 | Seats under the 16+ rules (`Tech.md` §10.6) | No |
| **The castle and the plaza** | Builds and shapes them by hand; they are royal (founder decision) | A segment | The castle also grows with the Town Hall | No |

Smaller acts, one press each: bless a home, visit a home
with the director following, name a street, change the sky, give a signpost.

**The dragon is earned, never called.** It comes only from Twitch moments and chat's own effort, such as the Wyrm Bell
(founder decision). The throne shows when the bell is close, so the monarch can make room for it.

### 7.4 The safety keys

On every form of the throne, for the monarch and the Watch alike:
- **Off the stream.** Takes the thing on screen off the stream view and sends it to the Watch, with the moment
  attached (`Safety.md` §2, principle 1).
- **Quiet the stream.** Until lifted, the director airs nothing citizens made tonight (`Safety.md` §2, principle 1).
- **The shutter.** For a raid gone wrong or a voice out of line: the stream view cuts to a calm wide shot with no
  names, no made things and no voices, in under a second, until lifted. Streamers built their own panic buttons
  during the 2021 hate raids ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2021/9/29/22700937)). Chatdom ships one.

### 7.5 Automatic or a choice

| Always automatic | Automatic, can be switched off | Always the monarch's own press |
|---|---|---|
| Lights and homes by tenure; sub, gift, cheer and raid spectacles; the Hype Train and its dragon; the communal pool and its level-ups; the director; the Ballad, unless struck; "earlier tonight" replays; the Chronicle | Stream markers at each chapter of the night; the Herald's lines; Busy by category; the moments that open the stage by themselves | Founding, decrees, raids, knighting, VIP, audiences, the wonder's design, crowning, festivals, the forge's roar, naming, King's Pick, weather, shoutouts, the campfire, Show |

The kingdom never makes Twitch clips; the Gallery hangs the director's own stills (founder decision). Stream markers
let streamers who cut VODs into videos find the night's moments without pop-ups in the footage, the complaint that made
one streamer switch channel points off ([Mike Matei](https://mikematei.com/blog/channel-points-are-gone/)).

### 7.6 The monarch in the world

- **The castle** is the monarch's home: the monarch has the castle, not a house (`Twitch.md` §12). Genesis raises it,
  the Town Hall grows it, and the monarch can build it by hand. The castle and the plaza are royal ground; citizens
  shape their own streets within the kit (founder decision, `World-Tree.md` §5). The castle also acts as the
  monarch's home for coals: they earn and spend coals in their own kingdom like any citizen, though the castle is not
  one of their three homes.
- **The throne room** is the stage for audiences and knightings, with voice under the 16+ rules and the streamer
  seated.
- **The beacon** is the monarch's light. It catches when the stream goes live.
- **The avatar** appears in every copy of a busy town (`Tech.md` §6.2). When the streamer is not driving it, it sits
  on the throne, and appears only at acts the monarch pressed: on the dais to knight, at the gate to ride out (founder
  decision). It never fights, greets or speaks on its own.
- **Walking.** A monarch can walk the kingdom, on stream or off. Citizens see their monarch arrive, and heads turn
  (`Artstyle-Soul-Feel.md` §8).

## 8. The Watch: what moderators do

### 8.1 Offices follow Twitch

`Twitch.md` §10 owns the offices: moderators are the Watch, a lead moderator is a Captain of the Watch, VIPs are
Nobles. A Twitch editor has no office in the world. Offices change live. **Wardens** are trusted citizens the monarch
may appoint to decide holds, and only holds (founder decision, `Safety.md` §5.4).

### 8.2 What the Watch does, and from where

| Act | Who | From where | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timeout, ban, warn | The Watch | Twitch, as always | The world follows within a second (`Tech.md` §13) |
| Hush or Eject | The Watch | The Watch drawer, chat, or in the world | For behaviour that does not deserve a Twitch timeout (`docs/08-depth-social.md` §3.15) |
| Hide and revert things | The Watch | The Watch drawer, or on the thing itself | Always with a reason and a fix (`Safety.md` §5.6) |
| Decide a hold: a Forge item, a name, a door-sign sentence, a game | The Watch and Wardens | The Watch drawer: one card, one thumb, or keys 1 to 4 | Let it stand, send it back, veil and note, or ask the monarch (`Safety.md` §5.4). Door signs carry one free sentence, judged first by a model (founder decision) |
| The safety keys | The Watch | Every form of the throne | Under a second. Wardens do not have them |
| Set the card's notice and raid welcome | The Watch | The Watch drawer | `Atlas.md` §3. These are not royal acts |
| Mute or unseat a voice | The Watch | The campfire's seat list | Logged with a reason |

No mod or Warden presses a royal act. Nobody but the monarch can raid, knight, crown, decree or call a festival
(founder decision). Mods cannot change a home beyond hiding and reverting it, or change the kingdom's rules, and there
is nothing private for them to see: no direct messages and no private voice exist. Every act lands in one log the
monarch and the Watch can read.

### 8.3 By size

| | 5 viewers | 500 viewers | 50,000 viewers |
|---|---|---|---|
| Who watches | The streamer and the judges | 2 to 5 mods, perhaps a Warden | A team with Captains and Wardens; reports weighted by trust; the stream pass on by default |
| Their queue | Almost empty | A few cards a night | The judges carry most of it |
| What they gain | A kingdom safe without them | Twitch-like tools in one place | Fewer incidents, because nothing unchecked airs |

The design gives mods fewer jobs, not more. Twitch's Predictions spread in big channels once mods could run them
during gameplay ([Twitch's Predictions designer](https://larsenpro.com/work/predictions)). Chatdom keeps royal acts
for the monarch alone, so it leans on timing (§3, principle 3) and on mods for safety, which is where busy streamers
need them most. The real risk is that busy streamers in big channels use fewer royal powers; suggestions at natural
pauses are the answer.

### 8.4 When things go wrong

- **Shield Mode closes the gate.** Making goes quiet and the gate closes to newcomers and tourists until it ends
  (`Safety.md` §5.8, `Twitch.md` §10). On the stream view, new arrivals show as unnamed lights.
- **A troll raid.** Raiders' makings pass the same judges (`Safety.md` §6, case 8). If it turns ugly, the shutter
  makes the stream safe while mods use Twitch's tools.
- **A kingdom with no mods,** most tiny ones. Uncertain things stay visible only to their maker until someone looks,
  the monarch sees the count at go-live, and Chatdom's staff act on the floor when nobody else does (`Safety.md`
  §5.4).

## 9. Over time

### 9.1 A night

| When | What the kingdom does | What the monarch might do |
|---|---|---|
| Before going live | Keeps the streamer's hours: dawn, the keepers at work, the market cart | Nothing |
| Going live | Dusk, the beacon, the starting scene filling with lights; the forge fire lit | Read names; glance at waiting holds |
| During play | Homes publish, forges finish, spectacles land in the window | Busy, if needed |
| A break | The stage tours the night, replays what was missed, shows the pool rising | Perhaps a decree |
| A big moment | The dragon, a raid arriving, a wonder stage, a pool level | Show, or nothing if it opens by itself |
| A kingdom night | Building, tables, Crown vs Crowd, the campfire | Start a game; sit at the fire |
| The end | Offers the Raid Gate | Open the gate |
| Offline | The Ballad, the rest, the Chronicle; the forge fire banked | Strike a line, or nothing |

A good night needs no presses; a full one is about 3 to 6. Only a live broadcast wakes the kingdom. A rerun or a
playlist does not, so a channel running reruns all day cannot farm light.

### 9.2 A week, a season, a year

- **A week:** game night on the weekday the streamer picks; a Chronicle for each stream, in words and the director's
  stills (`Moments.md` §8); the league ticks on Sunday; and a weekly letter on the throne, by email if wanted: who moved in, who came
  back, what was built, who visited. Never a list of tasks.
- **A season** of about 13 weeks (`docs/09` §2.10): the monarch chooses the wonder's design; festivals run
  themselves; contests with friends' kingdoms are opt-in; the Long Night closes the season with the crowning on the
  Atlas and the monarch speaking from the castle steps; the season's Chronicle follows.
- **A year:** Founding Day shows the director's stills of the founding night beside the kingdom as it is now. That before-and-after is the strongest thing Chatdom gives a streamer to show.

### 9.3 Offline

The kingdom rests but stays open (`ChatDom.md` §5). Citizens walk, build and play. The forge works only while the
stream is live (founder decision), so offline its fire is banked, and the first forge of each stream is a small
moment. The monarch can visit any time, and citizens see them arrive. Honours given offline are celebrated on the next
stream, and acts that write to Twitch wait for a live one. The phone throne shows the letter and nothing urgent.

### 9.4 Pride, clips and the flex

The moments that get clipped by viewers: the founding, the first night every window lights, a knighting with a name
read aloud, the dragon falling to the whole chat, the procession to a friend with its road drawn, the Ballad's eight
lines with real names, and a small kingdom crowned in its league.

How a monarch shows off to other streamers:
- **the kingdom's page** (`chatdom.gg/<login>`) with its postcard
- **the roads** on the Atlas, proof of friendships, and raid trains as named lines (`Atlas.md` §5)
- **the monarch's own card,** which like every card shows every home they hold and a public passport of the raids
  they rode (founder decision)
- **the league table,** inside their size, and Kingdom of the Week on the front page
- **a royal visit:** inviting another streamer in as a guest of honour, on stream or in shared chat, with citizens
  lining the street. The guest carries their own banner, never a crown (`World-Tree.md` rule 9)

A person may live in at most three places (founder decision). A monarch's castle does not count as one of them, so a
monarch may hold three homes elsewhere (founder decision).

## 10. Resting, leaving and handing over

**Resting.** For a holiday or a break, the castle's banners are furled and a notice says the monarch is travelling.
Citizens still visit, build and play. Nothing decays, and citizens' streaks count streams, not days, so none breaks.
The next go-live is a homecoming.

**A new name.** Kingdoms are keyed by the Twitch user ID, so a renamed channel keeps its kingdom and old links
redirect (`Tech.md` §8.8).

**Shared and duo channels.** A channel has one broadcaster account, and only that account sits on the throne.

**Laying down the crown,** from Settings or by revoking Chatdom on Twitch (founder decision):
- The kingdom sleeps. It stops reading Twitch and goes dark, but stands and stays visitable. Every home stands, and
  every citizen keeps what they made and earned.
- Twitch data is deleted as Twitch requires (`Tech.md` §7.5).
- The monarch can come back and pick up the crown, and the kingdom wakes as it was.
- On a request to delete, the kingdom's name, crest and castle go, and each home folds into its owner's keepsakes.
- Leaving is never punished, guilted or slowed.

A channel Twitch removes leaves the Atlas as `Atlas.md` §9 sets out, and its kingdom sleeps the same way.

## 11. What we never do to a streamer

- Charge for the kingdom, or for anything needed to rule it.
- Post in their chat beyond the Herald's rare lines, whisper their viewers, or advertise Chatdom in their channel.
- Make clips in their channel.
- Take their screen without their press or their standing choice, add a watermark, or cover their camera or game.
- Play sound they did not allow, or music not cleared for VODs.
- Cost them frames they can notice, or keep drawing when hidden.
- Put anything unchecked, any secret or any sanction on their stream.
- Show a viewer count, or a number that makes a small stream look empty.
- Make anything depend on them remembering, or give them streaks, chores or guilt.
- Speak in their name, or move their avatar on its own. Nothing imitates their voice or face, and nobody else presses
  a royal act.
- Draft their kingdom behind their back. It is born live, in front of their community.
- Ask for every permission at once.
- Reach into the game they are playing.
- Show a simulated crowd as if it were real (`Tech.md` §14).
- Rank them against bigger streamers, or by money.
- Hold their community hostage, or give their mods more work than before.
