This policy covers chatdom.io, the kingdoms you can visit there, and the Chatdom panel extension on Twitch.

In short

  • We never see your Twitch password. When you sign in, Twitch tells us your Twitch user id and your public display name, nothing more.
  • Chat messages show in a kingdom for a few seconds and are not stored. Only the words of a !forge wish stay, with the thing they made.

  • We use no analytics, no advertising and no tracking. Our fonts and scripts come from our own server.
  • You can ask us at any time what we hold about you, and ask us to remove it, by email.

Visiting the website

Chatdom runs with our hosting provider, Hetzner Online GmbH, on servers in Germany. When you open a page, your browser sends our server what it needs to deliver it: your IP address, the address of the page, the date and time, and technical details such as your browser type.

Our own application does not store IP addresses. When a request fails, it logs only the kind of request and the page address. The reverse proxy in front of it may keep access logs that include IP addresses; we use them only for security and to find faults, never combine them with other data, and keep them only as long as that needs.

Fonts come from our own server. There are no analytics, advertising, tracking or error-reporting services, and nothing loads from other companies except the Twitch pictures described below.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest is to deliver the website to you reliably and securely.

Cookies and storage in your browser

We set only two cookies, and only when you sign in:

  • __Host-chatdom_session keeps you signed in. It lasts 14 days, or until you sign out.
  • __Host-chatdom_signin protects the sign-in against forgery while you are on Twitch's page. It lasts at most 10 minutes and is removed as soon as you come back.

Both can only be read by our server over a secure connection, never by scripts on the page.

Our pages also keep a few small notes in your browser so that the parts you use work:

  • Until you close the tab: your seat in a kingdom, so you can reconnect after a dropped connection (removed when you sign out); where you were on the page before signing in, so we can bring you back (read once, kept at most 15 minutes); which place you opened on the front page; which view of the Atlas you were looking at.
  • Until you clear it: pieces you placed while practising with the Forge, the draft of a build you are working on, and whether you muted the panel extension.
  • A cache: up to six generated lands, so your browser does not have to draw them again.

None of this tracks you. Because all of it is strictly necessary for a service you ask for, no consent is required (§ 25 (2) no. 2 of the German Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act, TDDDG), and we show no cookie banner. Where personal data is involved, the legal basis is Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR.

Signing in with Twitch

Chatdom has no passwords of its own. You sign in on Twitch's own page, and we ask Twitch only for the openid permission, the smallest one there is. Twitch then tells us two things: your Twitch user id and your public display name. We do not receive your email address or your profile picture, and we cannot act on your Twitch account.

The access keys Twitch hands us during sign-in are thrown away at once; they are never stored or logged.

What we keep:

  • Your person record: your Twitch user id, your display name, a note that the name came from Twitch, and when you first and last signed in.
  • Your sessions: a scrambled fingerprint (hash) of your session key, your user id, and when the session ends or was ended. We do not store your IP address or details of your browser.
  • Sign-in attempts: fingerprints of the security values that tie Twitch's answer to your browser, and the page to return you to.

We clean up every ten minutes: sign-in attempts are deleted an hour after they expire, one-time entry tickets (valid for 60 seconds) a day after they expire, and sessions 30 days after they expire or you sign out. Your person record stays while you use Chatdom, and we delete it when you ask us to.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR, because you ask us to sign you in. On its sign-in page, Twitch acts under its own responsibility; see the Twitch privacy notice.

Chat in a founded kingdom

A streamer can found a Chatdom kingdom for their channel. While the channel is connected, Chatdom reads that channel's Twitch chat and events through Twitch's official interfaces, so that the chat can live in the kingdom. This happens whether or not you have signed in to Chatdom. The data comes from Twitch.

When you write in chat

We receive your Twitch user id and display name. You appear in the kingdom by name, and a window lights up for you. Your first message in a kingdom gives you a home there, with your name on it.

Your message shows above you as a speech bubble (up to 240 characters) for about ten seconds. It is not stored: it is never written into the kingdom's history, its saved states or our database. When a moderator deletes a message or clears chat on Twitch, Chatdom receives that notice and follows it. The one exception is a !forge wish, explained below.

When you only watch

Once a minute, Chatdom reads Twitch's list of who is in the channel's chat. From it we use only user ids, without names. If you watch without writing, you appear as an unnamed lit window, and nobody in the kingdom can see who you are. We keep a record of these observations: the ids, without names.

Your home and how long you have belonged

Your home comes with a deed: your user id, your name, the home, and when you received it. The deed also records evidence of how long you have belonged to the channel, taken from Twitch: when you followed and how many months you have subscribed. This evidence is signed so it cannot be forged.

Moments from Twitch

What happens on the channel becomes a moment in the kingdom, and moments are kept in the kingdom's history:

  • cheers: who cheered and how many Bits;
  • subscriptions and gifted subscriptions: who, the tier and the months;
  • Hype Trains: the names of those who took part;
  • raids: the raiding channel and how many viewers came along;
  • follows: who followed and when;
  • channel point redemptions: who redeemed which of Chatdom's rewards (never the text you type into a redemption);
  • polls: the question and how the votes fell.

Who sees it

A kingdom is public. Anyone who visits it, and everyone watching the stream, can see the names of people present, the names on homes and plaques, and the moments in its history, just as they can see names in the channel's Twitch chat.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest, and that of the streamer and their community, is to let the channel's chat live in the kingdom the streamer chose for it. We keep this light: we use only what is already public in the chat, keep no chat messages, and show watchers without names. Once you have signed in, showing you your own home is also part of the service you ask for (Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR).

If you do not want to be part of a kingdom, write to us and we will take your name out of it. See also your right to object.

If you found a kingdom

To found a kingdom, a streamer grants Chatdom permissions on Twitch: to be present in the channel's chat, to see who is in chat, and to receive cheers, Hype Trains, follows, channel point redemptions and polls. Chatdom may also create its own channel point rewards and open polls on the channel. We record which permissions you granted. The kingdom carries your channel's name, and what you do from the throne is recorded with your Twitch user id.

You can take the permissions back at any time in your Twitch settings under Connections. Chatdom then stops reading your channel and deletes its list of which window belongs to which viewer. The kingdom and its history stay until you ask us to remove them.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR, because you ask us to run a kingdom for your channel.

The Forge and our AI provider

In a founded kingdom, you can write !forge and a few words in chat while the stream is live, and the Forge makes a thing for your home from them. Where the Forge is offered on our website, you can wish there too.

The Forge uses AI models from OpenAI: AI safety judges check your words, a model makes the thing, and the judges check a rendered picture of the result before anyone else sees it. For this, OpenAI receives:

  • the words of your wish;
  • the text the model writes for the thing, such as its name and plaque;
  • a rendered picture of the result.

We do not send your name or your Twitch account to OpenAI. Where a name would appear, the model sees only a placeholder. Please do not put personal details about yourself or others into a wish.

What we keep: the words of a wish that passes are kept with the thing they made, as part of it, and the thing carries your name in the kingdom. The safety checks keep only scrambled fingerprints (hashes) of what they judged, not the words. A wish made on our website is sent without any identity, together with the style and home layout you chose, and kept in a log of Forge runs.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR for making the thing you wish for. For the safety checks, Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR: our legitimate interest, and that of everyone in the kingdom and on the stream, is that nothing hurtful is made or shown. OpenAI may process the data in the United States (see transfers). More in the OpenAI privacy policy.

The Chatdom panel on Twitch

A streamer can add the Chatdom panel extension below their stream. Twitch gives the panel the channel's id, your role (for example viewer or moderator) and a random viewer id that does not tell us who you are.

Your real Twitch user id reaches us only if you choose to share your identity with the extension. Twitch asks you, and we only offer it when you open your own citizen card. You can withdraw the share at any time in Twitch's extension settings.

The panel only reads: it writes nothing about you. To show your card, it reads your homes and name from Chatdom and asks Twitch for your public profile and your chat name colour. We keep these Twitch answers in memory for 10 minutes and then forget them. Whether you muted the panel is kept in your browser. The panel loads Twitch's own extension script from Twitch's servers, as every extension must.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (b) GDPR when you open your card and share your identity; otherwise Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in showing the panel to the channel's viewers. Twitch is responsible for how it runs extensions; see the Twitch privacy notice.

The Atlas

The Atlas at /map is a living map of Twitch. To draw it, we collect public channel data from Twitch's interface, about streamers, not viewers:

  • channel id, login name and display name;
  • broadcast language, stream title and game;
  • whether the channel is live, its current and peak viewer counts, and how long it has been live;
  • the address of the channel's profile picture;
  • raids between channels, with how many viewers came along.

Nothing about individual viewers goes into the Atlas. What you type into the Atlas search is not stored or logged.

Profile picture addresses and stream details are dropped after 24 hours. A channel that is no longer seen keeps its place on the map for 30 days. We keep only the three latest versions of the map and a few recent live frames.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest, and that of viewers, is a map that helps people find live streams; streamers publish this data on Twitch to be found. We collect only what Twitch shows everyone.

If you are a streamer and want your channel hidden from the Atlas, email us at tk@saartech.de with your channel name, and we will hide it.

Pictures from Twitch's servers

Some pictures we show are hosted by Twitch, for example the emotes in speech bubbles in a kingdom. Your browser loads them directly from Twitch's image servers (static-cdn.jtvnw.net). Twitch therefore receives your IP address and the technical details your browser sends, and processes them under its own responsibility. See the Twitch privacy notice.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR. Our legitimate interest is to show chat as people wrote it, emotes included.

When you write to us

When you email us, we use your address and what you write to handle your request, such as a report or a question about your data. We keep the conversation as long as that needs, and longer only where the law requires us to keep business letters.

Legal basis: Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR, our legitimate interest in answering you; where you ask about your rights, Art. 6 (1) (c) GDPR.

Transfers outside the EU

OpenAI may process the Forge data in the United States. This transfer takes place on the basis of the EU–US Data Privacy Framework and/or the EU Standard Contractual Clauses (Art. 45 and Art. 46 (2) (c) GDPR).

Twitch processes data in the United States under its own responsibility. When you sign in on Twitch's page, use the panel on Twitch or load a Twitch picture, you deal with Twitch directly. Twitch's privacy notice explains the safeguards it uses.

How long we keep data

  • Chat messages: not stored. A bubble lasts about ten seconds. To avoid showing a message twice, we remember only its id, for 10 minutes.
  • Sign-in and sessions: as described under signing in; sessions are deleted 30 days after they end.
  • Your person record: while you use Chatdom, or until you ask us to delete it.
  • Names, homes, deeds, moments, Forge wishes and presence records in a kingdom: as long as the kingdom exists, or until you ask us to remove them.
  • A streamer's list of window places: until the streamer takes Chatdom's permissions back.
  • Atlas data: profile picture addresses and stream details 24 hours; a channel's place 30 days after it was last seen.
  • The panel's Twitch lookups: 10 minutes, in memory.

Some of a kingdom's history is shared: a moment or a street belongs to everyone who was there. When you ask us to delete your data, we delete what is only yours. Where your data is woven into that shared history, we remove your name and Twitch id from it, so that it no longer points to you.

Your rights

Under the GDPR you have the right:

  • to know what data we hold about you and to receive a copy (Art. 15);
  • to have wrong data corrected (Art. 16);
  • to have your data deleted (Art. 17);
  • to have its use restricted (Art. 18);
  • to receive data you gave us in a common, machine-readable format (Art. 20);
  • to object to processing based on legitimate interest (Art. 21, see below).

To use any of these rights, email tk@saartech.de and tell us your Twitch name. We may ask you to show that the Twitch account is yours before we hand out or delete anything. We answer within one month.

You do not have to give us any data. Without signing in with Twitch, you cannot take a home through our website or see your own card; you can still look around.

Your right to object

Where we process your data on the basis of legitimate interest (Art. 6 (1) (f) GDPR), you have the right to object at any time, on grounds relating to your particular situation (Art. 21 GDPR). This covers, for example, your presence in a kingdom, the moments you were part of, and a streamer's channel on the Atlas.

Write to tk@saartech.de. We will then stop, unless we have compelling legitimate grounds that outweigh your interests, or we need the data to establish, exercise or defend legal claims.

AI and automated decisions

We make no decisions about you based solely on automated processing that have legal effects on you or affect you in a similarly significant way (Art. 22 GDPR).

The Forge uses AI models to make things from words. AI safety judges check each wish before anything is made, and the result before anyone else sees it. They may let it pass, keep it private until the channel's moderators have looked at it, or refuse it, and the Forge then makes nothing from that wish. These checks judge the thing, not the person: they never see your name or your history, and they never touch your home, your Twitch account or your place in the kingdom.

Children

Chatdom is used through Twitch, so Twitch's own minimum age applies: you must be old enough to use Twitch where you live. We do not ask for your age, and we do not knowingly collect more data from children than described here. If you think a child's data should be removed, write to us.
